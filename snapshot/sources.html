Courts and tribunals

Judgments, orders and advisory opinions. The highest evidentiary class in this record: findings of fact and law by the bodies competent to make them. Domestic judgments are listed here only where a question raised in this record has actually been litigated to judgment in a national court.

  • Legal Consequences of the Construction of a Wall in the Occupied Palestinian Territory
    International Court of Justice · 9 July 2004
    Advisory Opinion. Held the wall and the settlement enterprise contrary to international law and affirmed the applicability of the Fourth Geneva Convention to the occupied territory.
  • Application of the Genocide Convention (South Africa v. Israel) — Order on Provisional Measures
    International Court of Justice · 26 January 2024
    Found it plausible that the acts complained of fall within the Genocide Convention; ordered Israel to prevent and punish direct and public incitement to genocide. Measures strengthened 28 March and 24 May 2024.
  • Legal Consequences arising from the Policies and Practices of Israel in the Occupied Palestinian Territory, including East Jerusalem
    International Court of Justice · 19 July 2024
    Advisory Opinion. The occupation is unlawful and must end as rapidly as possible; settlements must be dismantled; reparations paid. Paragraph 229 found a breach of Article 3 CERD (racial segregation and apartheid).
  • Arrest warrants against Benjamin Netanyahu and Yoav Gallant
    International Criminal Court, Pre-Trial Chamber I · 21 November 2024
    Reasonable grounds to believe both bear responsibility for the war crime of starvation as a method of warfare and crimes against humanity of murder, persecution and other inhumane acts. A warrant was also issued against Hamas commander Mohammed Deif.
  • Situation in the State of Palestine
    International Criminal Court, Office of the Prosecutor · 2021–
    The open investigation covering conduct in the West Bank, East Jerusalem and Gaza since 13 June 2014.
  • Bosnia and Herzegovina v. Serbia and Montenegro
    International Court of Justice · 26 February 2007
    The only ICJ ruling directly on the duty to prevent genocide. The obligation arises the moment a state learns of a serious risk, not once genocide is proven; it is a duty of conduct, and it is irrelevant whether full effort would have succeeded.
  • Prosecutor v. Akayesu (ICTR-96-4-T)
    International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda · 2 September 1998
    The foundational jurisprudence on inferring genocidal intent from a pattern of conduct and from dehumanising speech.
  • University of Bristol v Dr David Miller [2026] EAT 84
    Employment Appeal Tribunal (England and Wales) · 4 August 2026
    Appeal EA-2024-000324-RS. Lord Fairley KC, with Nick Aziz and Dr Gillian Smith MBE. Eight of nine grounds rejected; anti-Zionist belief in the formulation advanced held a protected philosophical belief, dismissal held direct discrimination. Compensation reduced by 50 per cent for contributory conduct.
  • HCJ 5555/18, Hasson v. Knesset (petitions against Basic Law: Israel — the Nation State of the Jewish People)
    Supreme Court of Israel, sitting as the High Court of Justice · 8 July 2021
    An extended bench of eleven justices rejected fifteen petitions by ten to one, President Esther Hayut holding that the Basic Law does not negate the state's democratic character. The sole dissent was that of Justice George Karra, the court's only Arab justice.
  • State of New South Wales v Farhat [2026] NSWSC 985
    Supreme Court of New South Wales (Fagan J) · 30 July 2026, reasons 17 August 2026
    Refusal of an extended supervision order under the Terrorism (High Risk Offenders) Act 2017 (NSW). The court declined to take the meaning of antisemitism from the IHRA working definition, holding the text “so verbose and imprecise that it could not realistically be attributed to the ordinary, reasonably informed member of the Australian public” and “detached from the reality of the common usage and understanding of antisemitism in this country” (§21.9). The New South Wales Attorney General announced on 31 August 2026 that the State would not appeal.
  • Baldassi and Others v. France, applications nos. 15271/16 and six others
    European Court of Human Rights (Fifth Section) · 11 June 2020
    Unanimous finding that the criminal conviction of eleven activists for calling in supermarkets for a boycott of Israeli goods violated Article 10 of the Convention; EUR 7,380 awarded to each applicant. The Court held that a call to boycott is expression on a matter of public interest and distinguished Willem v. France (2009), where the applicant acted in the exercise of public authority as a mayor. Cited at Rebuttal 22.
  • NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co., 458 U.S. 886 (1982)
    Supreme Court of the United States · 2 July 1982
    Held that a politically motivated boycott is protected expression under the First Amendment, and that liability could not be imposed for peaceful advocacy of it. The domestic counterpart to Baldassi, and the authority on which several federal courts have enjoined state anti-boycott certification requirements. Cited at Rebuttal 22.
  • Obligations of Israel in relation to the Presence and Activities of the United Nations, Other International Organizations and Third States in and in relation to the Occupied Palestinian Territory
    International Court of Justice · 22 October 2025
    Advisory Opinion, General List No. 196 (UN doc. A/80/502), requested by General Assembly resolution 79/232. Unanimous on most findings, ten to one on the rest. Holds that Article 59 of the Fourth Geneva Convention obliges the occupying power to agree to and facilitate relief, that it must use all means at its disposal to see relief distributed fairly, and that UNRWA's capacity cannot be replicated or replaced on short notice. Recorded at §15.1A.

How this dashboard was built

No gaps

build.py parses the report into structured JSON, emitting every heading, paragraph, list item and table. verify.py then reconciles every non-blank line of the source against the output; it currently reports zero missing fragments. The Evidence section renders that JSON in full, so what you read there is the report itself, not a summary of it.

Time-series data

fetch_timeseries.py downloads the Tech For Palestine daily datasets (1,070 daily records, October 2023 – September 2026) and aggregates them to monthly series. Their cumulative totals corroborate the figures cited independently in the report: 73,922 killed as of 2026-09-23.

Corroboration

Every statistic must appear in at least two independent sources before it enters the record. Where sources disagree, the more recent and better-documented figure is used and the disagreement is stated. Where a figure is an estimate rather than a count — as with the mortality studies — it is labelled as one. Provenance joins every claim to the bodies it rests on, and will reject any class of source you name and recount what is left.
