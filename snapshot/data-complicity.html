Complicity

Who supplies the war

No state sustains a war of this scale alone. The weapons, the money and the diplomatic cover are supplied, and every supplier is named in the public record. This is the ledger of who supplies what — and of who has stopped.

Where Israel's major arms imports come from, 2020–2024

Share of major conventional arms deliveries. Two states account for ninety-nine per cent of them.

SIPRI

Interactive chart — open it in the live record.

The three suppliers

United States — 66%
Two thirds of Israel's major conventional arms imports. The relationship is not commercial in the ordinary sense: most of it is paid for with United States grant money, under the Foreign Military Financing programme, and spent back in the United States defence industry.
Germany — 33%
One third — the second largest supplier, and the largest in Europe. Germany approved roughly €326 million in arms exports to Israel in 2023, about ten times the 2022 figure. Berlin announced a partial suspension in August 2025 and lifted it in November 2025.
Italy — 0.6%
Italy suspended new export licences in October 2023 but continued deliveries under contracts signed before that date.

SIPRI measures deliveries of major conventional weapons, not components, spare parts or ammunition. The F-35 programme in particular is supplied through a global parts pool to which several states contribute without any bilateral licence appearing in their own figures — the mechanism the United Kingdom relied on when it suspended some direct licences in September 2024.

The suppliers' own numbers

What each government authorised, and when

SIPRI measures deliveries of major conventional weapons. The supplying states publish something different, and more specific: what they authorised, when, and to what value. Those three national records are set out here as each government reported them, including the German ministry's answer to a parliamentary question, the US congressional and Costs of War tallies, and the UK's own count of the licences it suspended against the ones it left in force.

German arms export licences to Israel, by year

The bar for 2022 is derived from the report's "tenfold increase" wording rather than separately published, and is drawn dashed for that reason. The 2026 bar is half a year. Chancellor Friedrich Merz suspended export licences for military equipment potentially usable in Gaza — the first German arms restriction on Israel. Berlin lifted it on 24 November 2025, citing the October 2025 ceasefire as having "stabilised". Amnesty International called the lifting "reckless, unlawful and risks complicity in Israel's international crimes".

What the United Kingdom suspended, and what it left in force

Roughly 30 of 350 extant export licences suspended over the risk of breaches of international humanitarian law. The remainder, including the disputed exemption for F-35 components, were left intact.

  • The legal position is not in doubt. Common Article 1 of the Geneva Conventions obliges every High Contracting Party to ensure respect for the Conventions; the Arms Trade Treaty, Article 6(3), prohibits any transfer where the state knows the arms would be used in war crimes; and the International Court of Justice's advisory opinion of 19 July 2024 held that all states are under an obligation not to render aid or assistance in maintaining the unlawful occupation.
  • On 20 June 2025 the United Nations Special Rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territory published a report naming the corporate entities she found to be sustaining it, and recommending that states and companies sever those ties.
Divestment

The settlement-business database, and what has moved

In 2016 the Human Rights Council instructed its own office to compile a database of businesses operating in the Israeli settlements. It is the only official list of its kind, and it has grown at every revision.

Businesses listed by the United Nations as operating in the settlements

Three releases in five years. The 2023 fall is not a reprieve: fifteen entities were removed because they stopped.

OHCHR, HRC resolution 31/36

Interactive chart — open it in the live record.

What listing means

Listing is not an accusation of a crime. It records that the entity carries on one of ten activities the Human Rights Council identified as raising particular human rights concerns in the settlements — supply of equipment for demolition, services sustaining settlement maintenance, banking for settlement construction, use of natural resources, and the rest. Israeli settlements in the occupied territory are unlawful under Article 49(6) of the Fourth Geneva Convention, a position the International Court of Justice restated on 19 July 2024.

Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights, database pursuant to Human Rights Council resolution 31/36

  • Divestment is not a boycott of a people. It is the withdrawal of investment from named commercial activity in territory an international court has held to be unlawfully occupied.
  • The precedent is exact and recent: the same instruments — disclosure, listing, divestment, procurement bans — were used against apartheid South Africa, and the states that used them are in many cases the same states that now decline to.
The veto wall

One state against fourteen

The Security Council is the only United Nations organ whose decisions bind. Since 1972 the United States has used its permanent-member veto to stop draft resolutions concerning Israel more often than on any other subject in the Council's history.

  • The pattern is the point. In every vote since November 2024 the tally has been fourteen to one: every other member of the Security Council, including every other permanent member and every American ally on the Council, voted for a ceasefire.
  • General Assembly resolutions on the same question have passed repeatedly by margins above 120 votes. They are not binding. That is the design the veto protects.

Among the thirty-six: the United States, Germany, Italy, Japan, South Korea, the Netherlands, Austria, Greece, Switzerland, Croatia, Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia, North Macedonia, Panama, Cameroon, Eritrea, and a group of Pacific microstates — Fiji, Kiribati, the Marshall Islands, Micronesia, Nauru, Palau, Samoa, Tonga and Tuvalu — whose votes at the General Assembly are reliably aligned with Washington.

  • Recognition is a legal act with consequences: it establishes the recognising state's position that Palestine satisfies the criteria of statehood, and it opens treaty relations, diplomatic representation and standing before international bodies.
  • It is also the measure on which the gap between governments and their populations is widest. The states that continue to withhold recognition are, with few exceptions, the states that continue to supply the weapons.
