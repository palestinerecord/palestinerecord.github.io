2 Nov 1917
Balfour Declaration
3 Jan 1919
Faisal–Weizmann Agreement signed, conditional on Arab independence
Faisal's handwritten reservation made the agreement void if Arab independence was not granted. It was not, and the agreement lapsed; it is nonetheless still cited as Arab consent.
28 Aug 1919
King–Crane Commission reports that the Zionist programme could be carried out only by force of arms
The American commission appointed by the Paris Peace Conference found that the non-Jewish population, then about 90% of Palestine, was emphatically against the programme, and that its realisation would require a military occupation. The report was suppressed until 1922.
4–7 Apr 1920
Nebi Musa riots in Jerusalem
The first mass communal violence of the Mandate period. The Palin Commission of Inquiry attributed Arab fears to the Balfour Declaration and to the belief that self-determination had been denied. Its report was never published.
19–26 Apr 1920
San Remo conference assigns Britain the Mandate for Palestine, incorporating the Balfour Declaration
A commitment made by one people to a second people over the land of a third was converted into an instrument of international law.
1–7 May 1921
Jaffa riots; the Haycraft Commission finds the cause is Arab fear of dispossession
47 Jews and 48 Arabs killed. The commission concluded that Arab hostility was not religious but arose from political and economic causes connected with Jewish immigration.
3 Jun 1922
Churchill White Paper affirms the Jewish national home while denying an intention to displace the Arab population
The first British attempt to reconcile two commitments that were not reconcilable on the ground.
24 Jul 1922
League of Nations approves the Mandate for Palestine
Article 2 obliged Britain to secure the establishment of the Jewish national home; the Arab majority is referred to only as the 'non-Jewish communities', and only its civil and religious — not political — rights are protected.
Nov 1923
Jabotinsky publishes 'The Iron Wall'
Argues that Zionist colonisation can proceed only behind an iron wall of force that the native population cannot break, because no people ever consents to its own displacement. Cited in the report as an accurate description of what followed.
1925
Brit Shalom founded in Jerusalem, arguing for a binational state
Founded at Arthur Ruppin's home after the 14th Zionist Congress; members included Martin Buber, Gershom Scholem, Hugo Bergmann, Hans Kohn and Henrietta Szold. It held that Palestine belonged to both peoples and that both were entitled to self-determination. Part of the Jewish dissent from political Zionism treated in §10 of the report; it disintegrated by mid-1936.
1929
Hebron massacre and Arab–Jewish riots
Mar 1930
Shaw Commission reports on the 1929 riots
Found the fundamental cause to be Arab landlessness and unemployment arising from Jewish land purchase and immigration.
21 Oct 1930
Hope Simpson report and the Passfield White Paper
Found that the cultivable land available could not support further large-scale settlement without displacing Arab cultivators. The White Paper's restrictions were reversed by the MacDonald Letter of February 1931 after Zionist political pressure in London.
1931
Irgun founded, splitting from the Haganah
The paramilitary organisation later designated a terrorist organisation by the British authorities; its commander from 1943 was Menachem Begin, later Prime Minister.
25 Aug 1933
Haavara (Transfer) Agreement signed with Nazi Germany
Enabled the emigration of German Jews to Palestine with part of their assets, and brought German goods into Palestine during the international boycott of Nazi Germany. Documented in the German Foreign Ministry archives; treated in §1.2A of the report.
20 Nov 1935
Izz ad-Din al-Qassam killed by British police near Ya'bad
His death precipitated the general strike and the Arab Revolt of 1936–39.
1936–39
British suppression of Arab Revolt with Haganah collaboration
19 Apr 1936
Arab general strike begins; the Arab Revolt opens
The longest general strike in colonial history to that point, demanding an end to Jewish immigration and land transfers and the establishment of representative government.
7 Jul 1937
Peel Commission recommends partition and the 'transfer' of the Arab population
The first official proposal for the compulsory removal of some 225,000 Arabs from the proposed Jewish state. The word transfer enters the official record here.
12 Jul 1937
Ben-Gurion's diary: the compulsory transfer of Arabs 'could give us something we never had'
The Ben-Gurion Archives at Sde Boker. Reproduced in Appendix C and §1.3 of the report.
1938
Special Night Squads formed under Orde Wingate with Haganah members
British-trained joint units used in the suppression of the revolt. Moshe Dayan and Yigal Allon served in them; the methods were carried into 1948.
17 May 1939
British White Paper limits Jewish immigration and land purchase
Proposed an independent Palestine within ten years with an Arab majority. Rejected by the Zionist leadership; the Irgun and Lehi campaigns against British rule follow.
28 Feb 1940
Land Transfer Regulations restrict Jewish land purchase to 5% of Palestine
By 1947, after four decades of purchase, Jewish ownership stood at about 6–7% of the land; the 1947 partition plan nonetheless allotted 55% of the territory to the Jewish state.
Jan 1941
Lehi proposes alliance to Nazi Germany (Ankara Memorandum)
11 May 1942
Biltmore Program demands a Jewish Commonwealth in the whole of Palestine
Adopted in New York; the movement's public abandonment of partition in favour of the whole territory.
6 Nov 1944
Lehi assassinates Lord Moyne
1945
British Defence (Emergency) Regulations enacted
Administrative detention, house demolition, closure and deportation powers. Denounced at the time by Jewish jurists in Palestine as Nazi-like; retained by Israel after 1948 and still applied in the occupied territory today.
22 Jul 1946
King David Hotel bombing - 91 killed
29 Nov 1947
UN General Assembly Resolution 181 recommends partition
Allotted 55% of the territory to a Jewish state whose population would have been 45% Arab, at a time when Jewish residents were about a third of the population and owned about 7% of the land.
10 Mar 1948
Plan Dalet adopted
The Haganah plan providing for the destruction of villages and the expulsion of their populations. Its execution began before the declaration of the state and before any Arab army entered Palestine.
9 Apr 1948
Deir Yassin massacre
14 May 1948
Declaration of the State of Israel
By this date roughly 300,000 Palestinians had already been displaced. The war with the Arab states began the following day.
22–23 May 1948
Tantura massacre
13 Jul 1948
Lydda Death March (Rabin orders expulsion)
17 Sept 1948
Lehi (incl. Shamir) assassinates UN mediator Bernadotte
29 Oct 1948
Dawayima massacre
11 Dec 1948
UN General Assembly Resolution 194 affirms the right of return
Resolved that refugees wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date. Reaffirmed by the General Assembly annually since.
11 May 1949
Israel admitted to the United Nations
Resolution 273 recalls Israel's undertakings to implement Resolutions 181 and 194. Neither was implemented.
14 Mar 1950
Absentees' Property Law transfers refugee property to the state
The legal mechanism by which the land and homes of the displaced were expropriated, including from 'present absentees' who remained inside Israel.
5 Jul 1950
Law of Return grants every Jew worldwide the right to immigrate
Read with the Absentees' Property Law and the 1952 Citizenship Law, it is the pairing that B'Tselem and Amnesty cite as the foundation of the demographic regime.
1953
Land Acquisition Law retroactively legalises the seizure of Arab land
Completed the transfer of expropriated property; by the mid-1950s the state and the Jewish National Fund controlled the great majority of the land.
14 Oct 1953
Qibya massacre (Sharon)
29 Oct 1956
Kafr Qasim massacre
3–12 Nov 1956
Khan Yunis and Rafah massacres
1948–1966
Military government over Palestinian citizens of Israel
Eighteen years of permits, curfews, closed areas and administrative detention applied to citizens on the basis of nationality. Abolished in November 1966, five months before the occupation of the West Bank and Gaza began.
23 Oct 1965
Israel's Supreme Court bars the Socialists' List from the Knesset election
EA 1/65 Yeredor. The Court upheld the exclusion of a list drawn from the banned al-Ard movement, although the Basic Law: The Knesset then contained no provision permitting it, on the reasoning that a purpose denying the state's existence is an unlawful purpose. The first judicial limit on the political rights of Palestinian citizens; Justice Haim Cohn dissented.
5–10 Jun 1967
Six-Day War; occupation of WB, Gaza, Golan, Sinai
Jul 1967
Allon Plan sets the template for settlement of the occupied territory
Proposed retaining the Jordan Valley and a chain of settlements; the geography of Area C follows it closely.
Sep 1967
Theodor Meron advises the Israeli government that civilian settlement violates the Fourth Geneva Convention
The Foreign Ministry's own legal counsel, in a memorandum to the Prime Minister's office: 'civilian settlement in the administered territories contravenes the explicit provisions of the Fourth Geneva Convention.' Settlement proceeded regardless. The strongest single admission in the settlement record.
27 Sep 1967
Kfar Etzion established — the first settlement in the occupied West Bank
Followed by more than 300 settlements and outposts and, by 2026, over 700,000 settlers.
22 Nov 1967
UN Security Council Resolution 242 emphasises the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war
The cornerstone of every subsequent negotiation, and the provision the ICJ relied on in its 2024 advisory opinion.
21 Aug 1969
Arson at Al-Aqsa Mosque; the Organisation of the Islamic Conference is founded in response
The fire, set by an Australian Christian acting alone on a millenarian conviction, destroyed Saladin's 12th-century minbar. UN Security Council Resolution 271 of 15 September 1969 condemned Israel's failure to observe earlier resolutions on Jerusalem; twenty-five states met at Rabat on 25 September and founded the OIC.
Jul–Aug 1971
Sharon's clearance operations in the Gaza refugee camps
Thousands of homes demolished to drive wide roads through the camps for military access; some 16,000 people displaced a second time.
6–25 Oct 1973
October War
Ended the post-1967 assumption that the territorial status quo was costless; it did not alter the status of the occupied territory.
30 Mar 1976
Land Day: six Palestinian citizens of Israel killed protesting land expropriation in the Galilee
The first general strike by Palestinian citizens of Israel, and the event that marked their emergence as a political community within the state.
1977–1978
Likud takes power; the Drobles Plan sets out settlement to prevent territorial contiguity
The World Zionist Organisation plan stated the aim of settling between and around Palestinian population centres so as to make a contiguous Palestinian territory impossible.
26 Mar 1979
Egypt–Israel peace treaty
Removed the strongest Arab military constraint on Israeli action in the occupied territory; the autonomy talks on the Palestinians led nowhere.
30 Jul 1980
Jerusalem Law annexes East Jerusalem
Declared null and void by UN Security Council Resolution 478 in August 1980; the annexation has never been recognised in international law.
1981
Illegal annexation of Golan Heights
6 Jun 1982
Invasion of Lebanon
An estimated 17,000–20,000 killed, the great majority civilians, during the invasion and the siege of Beirut.
16–18 Sep 1982
Sabra and Shatila massacre
8 Feb 1983
Kahan Commission finds Ariel Sharon personally responsible for Sabra and Shatila
Israel's own commission of inquiry found he bore personal responsibility for ignoring the danger of bloodshed. He resigned as Defence Minister and became Prime Minister eighteen years later.
4 Aug 1985
The 'iron fist' policy in the occupied territory
Under Defence Minister Yitzhak Rabin: administrative detention restored, deportations resumed on a large scale, and house demolition extended beyond cases of killing or wounding. The UN Special Committee to Investigate Israeli Practices recorded the escalation at the time. Rabin was quoted as saying the purpose was to make life so difficult that the population would leave. The First Intifada began two years later.
9 Dec 1987
First Intifada begins
Jan 1988
The 'force, might and beatings' policy in the First Intifada
Defence Minister Yitzhak Rabin's instruction; some 1,000 Palestinians hospitalised with fractures in the first months, documented at the time by Israeli and international medical organisations.
15 Nov 1988
Palestinian Declaration of Independence accepts partition on the 1967 lines
The PLO's acceptance of a state on 22% of historic Palestine. Recognised within a year by more than 80 states.
8 Oct 1990
Al-Aqsa compound killings: 21 Palestinians shot dead
UN Security Council Resolution 672 condemned the killings; Israel refused entry to the Secretary-General's mission.
13 Sep 1993
Oslo I: Declaration of Principles signed in Washington
Deferred Jerusalem, refugees, settlements and borders to final-status talks that never concluded. The settler population roughly doubled during the Oslo years.
25 Feb 1994
Cave of the Patriarchs massacre - Goldstein
28 Sep 1995
Oslo II divides the West Bank into Areas A, B and C
Area C, some 60% of the West Bank and the location of nearly all its land reserves, remained under full Israeli control — an arrangement intended to last five years and still in force after thirty.
4 Nov 1995
Yitzhak Rabin assassinated by Yigal Amir
Killed by an Israeli religious-nationalist opposed to the Oslo Accords, after a campaign of incitement in which Benjamin Netanyahu was a prominent figure.
18 Apr 1996
Qana massacre
17 Jan 1997
Hebron Protocol divides the city into H1 and H2
80% of Hebron transferred to the Palestinian Authority; the 20% containing the Old City, the Ibrahimi Mosque, four settlements and Shuhada Street kept under Israeli control, where some 7,000 Palestinians live alongside 500–800 settlers and about 1,500 soldiers. The Protocol required Shuhada Street to be reopened; it was fenced off entirely in 2002 and Palestinians remain barred from it. Smotrich announced the Protocol's cancellation in June 2026.
23 Oct 1998
Wye River Memorandum
A further redeployment agreement brokered at Wye Plantation; implementation was suspended by Israel two months later. The last of the Oslo-era agreements before the collapse at Camp David.
11–25 Jul 2000
