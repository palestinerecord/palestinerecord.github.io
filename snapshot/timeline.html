14 Jul 1915
The Hussein–McMahon correspondence opens
Ten letters between Sharif Hussein of Mecca and the British High Commissioner in Egypt, Sir Henry McMahon, running to March 1916, in which Britain undertook to support Arab independence in return for a revolt against the Ottomans. Whether Palestine was inside or outside the promised area has been disputed ever since; Britain's own 1939 committee of inquiry could not agree, and the correspondence was made to sit alongside two later commitments that contradicted it.
16 May 1916
The Sykes–Picot Agreement divides the Ottoman Arab provinces
A secret Anglo-French understanding, concluded with Russian assent, allotting spheres of control and placing most of Palestine under an undefined international administration. It was signed while the Hussein–McMahon undertakings were still live, and was published by the Bolsheviks in November 1917, days after the Balfour Declaration.
2 Nov 1917
Balfour Declaration
3 Jan 1919
Faisal–Weizmann Agreement signed, conditional on Arab independence
Faisal's handwritten reservation made the agreement void if Arab independence was not granted. It was not, and the agreement lapsed; it is nonetheless still cited as Arab consent.
28 Jun 1919
Article 22 of the League Covenant creates the mandate system
The Covenant described mandated peoples as communities whose existence as independent nations could be provisionally recognised, and the mandatory as holding a sacred trust of civilisation until they could stand alone. Palestine was classed as a Class A mandate, the category nearest to independence — the legal premise against which the Mandate's administration is measured.
28 Aug 1919
King–Crane Commission reports that the Zionist programme could be carried out only by force of arms
The American commission appointed by the Paris Peace Conference found that the non-Jewish population, then about 90% of Palestine, was emphatically against the programme, and that its realisation would require a military occupation. The report was suppressed until 1922.
4–7 Apr 1920
Nebi Musa riots in Jerusalem
The first mass communal violence of the Mandate period. The Palin Commission of Inquiry attributed Arab fears to the Balfour Declaration and to the belief that self-determination had been denied. Its report was never published.
19–26 Apr 1920
San Remo conference assigns Britain the Mandate for Palestine, incorporating the Balfour Declaration
A commitment made by one people to a second people over the land of a third was converted into an instrument of international law.
Dec 1920
The Histadrut is founded, with Hebrew labour as policy
The General Federation of Jewish Labour organised the Yishuv's economy around the exclusive employment of Jewish workers and the exclusive purchase of Jewish produce. It was a labour policy in form and a separation policy in effect, and the campaigns it ran against Arab labour on Jewish-owned land are documented in its own records.
1–7 May 1921
Jaffa riots; the Haycraft Commission finds the cause is Arab fear of dispossession
47 Jews and 48 Arabs killed. The commission concluded that Arab hostility was not religious but arose from political and economic causes connected with Jewish immigration.
3 Jun 1922
Churchill White Paper affirms the Jewish national home while denying an intention to displace the Arab population
The first British attempt to reconcile two commitments that were not reconcilable on the ground.
24 Jul 1922
League of Nations approves the Mandate for Palestine
Article 2 obliged Britain to secure the establishment of the Jewish national home; the Arab majority is referred to only as the 'non-Jewish communities', and only its civil and religious — not political — rights are protected.
Nov 1923
Jabotinsky publishes 'The Iron Wall'
Argues that Zionist colonisation can proceed only behind an iron wall of force that the native population cannot break, because no people ever consents to its own displacement. Cited in the report as an accurate description of what followed.
4 Nov 1923
Jabotinsky publishes 'The Iron Wall'
Ze'ev Jabotinsky sets out the programme in terms: 'Zionist colonisation must either stop, or else proceed regardless of the native population. Which means that it can proceed and develop only under the protection of a power that is independent of the native population - behind an iron wall, which the native population cannot breach.' The clearest statement of the settler-colonial analysis of the project is a Zionist one, which is why Rebuttal 23 turns on it.
1 Aug 1925
The Palestinian Citizenship Order comes into force
The Order created a Palestinian citizenship for the inhabitants of the Mandate, held by Arabs and Jews alike. It governed until 14 May 1948 and was repealed by Israel's Nationality Law of 1952, which replaced it with a status the refugees of 1948 could not meet the conditions for.
1925
Brit Shalom founded in Jerusalem, arguing for a binational state
Founded at Arthur Ruppin's home after the 14th Zionist Congress; members included Martin Buber, Gershom Scholem, Hugo Bergmann, Hans Kohn and Henrietta Szold. It held that Palestine belonged to both peoples and that both were entitled to self-determination. Part of the Jewish dissent from political Zionism treated in §10 of the report; it disintegrated by mid-1936.
1929
Hebron massacre and Arab–Jewish riots
Mar 1930
Shaw Commission reports on the 1929 riots
Found the fundamental cause to be Arab landlessness and unemployment arising from Jewish land purchase and immigration.
21 Oct 1930
Hope Simpson report and the Passfield White Paper
Found that the cultivable land available could not support further large-scale settlement without displacing Arab cultivators. The White Paper's restrictions were reversed by the MacDonald Letter of February 1931 after Zionist political pressure in London.
1931
Irgun founded, splitting from the Haganah
The paramilitary organisation later designated a terrorist organisation by the British authorities; its commander from 1943 was Menachem Begin, later Prime Minister.
25 Aug 1933
Haavara (Transfer) Agreement signed with Nazi Germany
Enabled the emigration of German Jews to Palestine with part of their assets, and brought German goods into Palestine during the international boycott of Nazi Germany. Documented in the German Foreign Ministry archives; treated in §1.2A of the report.
20 Nov 1935
Izz ad-Din al-Qassam killed by British police near Ya'bad
His death precipitated the general strike and the Arab Revolt of 1936–39.
1936–39
British suppression of Arab Revolt with Haganah collaboration
19 Apr 1936
Arab general strike begins; the Arab Revolt opens
The longest general strike in colonial history to that point, demanding an end to Jewish immigration and land transfers and the establishment of representative government.
7 Jul 1937
Peel Commission recommends partition and the 'transfer' of the Arab population
The first official proposal for the compulsory removal of some 225,000 Arabs from the proposed Jewish state. The word transfer enters the official record here.
12 Jul 1937
Ben-Gurion's diary: the compulsory transfer of Arabs 'could give us something we never had'
The Ben-Gurion Archives at Sde Boker. Reproduced in Appendix C and §1.3 of the report.
1938
Neturei Karta founded in Jerusalem in rejection of Zionism on religious grounds
Rabbis Amram Blau and Aharon Katzenelbogen broke from the Jerusalem branch of Agudath Israel over its accommodation with the Zionist institutions of the Yishuv. The doctrinal basis is the Three Oaths of Ketubot 111a. A fringe group then and now, numbering in the hundreds to low thousands, and disowned across the Haredi world, including by Satmar (§23.1).
1938
Special Night Squads formed under Orde Wingate with Haganah members
British-trained joint units used in the suppression of the revolt. Moshe Dayan and Yigal Allon served in them; the methods were carried into 1948.
6-15 July 1938
The Évian Conference declines to admit Jewish refugees
Delegates of 32 states met at Évian-les-Bains to consider German and Austrian Jewish refugees. With the sole exception of the Dominican Republic, none agreed to raise its admissions. Britain attended on condition that Mandatory Palestine would not be discussed; the United States sent a businessman rather than a senior official and proposed no increase in its quotas. On 13 July the Nazi party newspaper Völkischer Beobachter drew the conclusion: "No one wants them."
7 Feb – 17 Mar 1939
The St James's Palace Conference meets in separate rooms
Britain convened the Arab and Zionist delegations at St James's Palace to settle Palestine's future; the Arab Higher Committee refused to sit with the Zionist delegation, so Colonial Secretary Malcolm MacDonald held parallel talks and two official photographs were taken. Both sides rejected the British proposals of 15 March, and Britain then imposed its own policy in the White Paper of May 1939.
17 May 1939
British White Paper limits Jewish immigration and land purchase
Proposed an independent Palestine within ten years with an Arab majority. Rejected by the Zionist leadership; the Irgun and Lehi campaigns against British rule follow.
23 May 1939
The White Paper caps Jewish immigration to Palestine
Britain limited Jewish immigration to 75,000 over five years and provided that further immigration would require the consent of the Arab majority; land transfer regulations the following year restricted Jewish land purchase in all but about 5 per cent of Palestine. The policy was rejected by the Zionist leadership and by the Palestine Arab parties alike. By 1944 only 51,000 of the 75,000 certificates had been used. The cap held through the years of the extermination in Europe.
28 Feb 1940
Land Transfer Regulations restrict Jewish land purchase to 5% of Palestine
By 1947, after four decades of purchase, Jewish ownership stood at about 6–7% of the land; the 1947 partition plan nonetheless allotted 55% of the territory to the Jewish state.
Jan 1941
Lehi proposes alliance to Nazi Germany (Ankara Memorandum)
11 May 1942
Biltmore Program demands a Jewish Commonwealth in the whole of Palestine
Adopted in New York; the movement's public abandonment of partition in favour of the whole territory.
7 Dec 1942
American Council for Judaism constituted in opposition to the establishment of a Jewish state
Formed by thirty-six Reform rabbis after a meeting at Atlantic City on 1 June 1942 opposing the CCAR resolution supporting a Jewish army in Palestine. It held that Judaism is a religion and not a nationality, and advocated a democratic Palestine with equal political rights irrespective of religion. The position had already been overturned inside the movement by the Columbus Platform of 1937 (§23.2).
6 Nov 1944
Lehi assassinates Lord Moyne
1945
British Defence (Emergency) Regulations enacted
Administrative detention, house demolition, closure and deportation powers. Denounced at the time by Jewish jurists in Palestine as Nazi-like; retained by Israel after 1948 and still applied in the occupied territory today.
22 Jul 1946
King David Hotel bombing - 91 killed
13 April 1947
The Theodor Herzl is intercepted off Palestine
The Haganah refugee ship reached the coast carrying about 2,600 Jewish passengers, most of them survivors of the Nazi camps, outside the British quota. A banner along the rail read: "The Germans destroyed our families and homes - don't you destroy our hopes." A Royal Navy boarding party took the ship, three passengers were killed resisting, and the passengers were deported to the British internment camps on Cyprus, through which Yad Vashem records 52,000 people passing between 1946 and 1949. The photograph of the banner is authentic and is frequently recaptioned; the interception was carried out by the Mandatory power, not by the population of Palestine.
31 Aug 1947
UNSCOP reports, and its majority recommends partition
The UN Special Committee on Palestine divided: a majority proposed partition with economic union, a minority a federal binational state. Neither plan was put to the population it concerned. The majority plan became the basis of Resolution 181 three months later.
29 Nov 1947
UN General Assembly Resolution 181 recommends partition
Allotted 55% of the territory to a Jewish state whose population would have been 45% Arab, at a time when Jewish residents were about a third of the population and owned about 7% of the land.
10 Mar 1948
Plan Dalet adopted
The Haganah plan providing for the destruction of villages and the expulsion of their populations. Its execution began before the declaration of the state and before any Arab army entered Palestine.
9 Apr 1948
Deir Yassin massacre
9 Apr 1948
Deir Yassin attacked by the Irgun and Lehi
More than a hundred villagers killed, five weeks before the regular armies of the Arab states crossed on 15 May. Roughly 300,000 Palestinians had been displaced by that date, which is the fact the account of a flight ordered by Arab leaders is offered to displace. Rebuttal 20.
14 May 1948
Declaration of the State of Israel
By this date roughly 300,000 Palestinians had already been displaced. The war with the Arab states began the following day.
22–23 May 1948
Tantura massacre
13 Jul 1948
Lydda Death March (Rabin orders expulsion)
17 Sept 1948
Lehi (incl. Shamir) assassinates UN mediator Bernadotte
29 Oct 1948
Dawayima massacre
11 Dec 1948
UN General Assembly Resolution 194 affirms the right of return
Resolved that refugees wishing to return to their homes and live at peace with their neighbours should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date. Reaffirmed by the General Assembly annually since.
24 Feb – 20 Jul 1949
The armistice agreements draw the Green Line
Separate agreements with Egypt (24 February), Lebanon (23 March), Jordan (3 April) and Syria (20 July) fixed the ceasefire lines that became the 1967 borders. Each agreement stated in terms that the line was not a political or territorial boundary and was without prejudice to a final settlement.
11 May 1949
Israel admitted to the United Nations
Resolution 273 recalls Israel's undertakings to implement Resolutions 181 and 194. Neither was implemented.
12 May 1949
Israel signs the Lausanne Protocol, and is admitted to the UN the next day
The protocol accepted the 1947 partition map as the working basis for talks and the return of refugees as a subject for them. Israel was admitted to the United Nations on 11 May 1949 on an undertaking to implement Resolutions 181 and 194; the Lausanne talks then produced nothing, and neither undertaking was carried out.
14 Mar 1950
Absentees' Property Law transfers refugee property to the state
The legal mechanism by which the land and homes of the displaced were expropriated, including from 'present absentees' who remained inside Israel.
24 Apr 1950
Jordan annexes the West Bank
The Jordanian parliament formally united the two banks and extended Jordanian nationality to the Palestinians under its control. Only Britain and Pakistan recognised the annexation; the Arab League treated the territory as a trust. The measure was undone in law only in 1988.
1 May 1950
UNRWA begins operations
Established by General Assembly Resolution 302 (IV) in December 1949, the agency started work with about 750,000 registered refugees. It was created as a temporary body pending a political settlement, and its mandate has been renewed ever since, which is the plainest measure of how little the settlement has advanced.
5 Jul 1950
Law of Return grants every Jew worldwide the right to immigrate
Read with the Absentees' Property Law and the 1952 Citizenship Law, it is the pairing that B'Tselem and Amnesty cite as the foundation of the demographic regime.
14 Jul 1952
The Nationality Law comes into force and repeals Palestinian citizenship
Passed by the Knesset on 1 April 1952 and in force from 14 July, the law made every Jew an Israeli national on immigration under the Law of Return, while a former Palestinian citizen acquired nationality only by proving registration on 1 March 1952 and continuous presence since the establishment of the state. Refugees who had fled or been expelled could satisfy neither condition, and the law repealed the Palestinian citizenship they had held since 1925.
1953
Land Acquisition Law retroactively legalises the seizure of Arab land
Completed the transfer of expropriated property; by the mid-1950s the state and the Jewish National Fund controlled the great majority of the land.
14 Oct 1953
Qibya massacre (Sharon)
1954
The Prevention of Infiltration Law criminalises refugee return
The statute made unauthorised entry by a person who had been in Palestine before 1948 a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment and deportation, and applied to the refugees attempting to return to their own villages. It converted the physical prevention of return into a permanent legal regime.
29 Oct 1956
Kafr Qasim massacre
29 Oct 1956
Israel invades Sinai in concert with Britain and France
The Suez war was preceded by the secret Protocol of Sèvres, in which the three governments agreed the Israeli attack would supply the pretext for an Anglo-French intervention. Israel withdrew from Sinai and Gaza in March 1957 under American and Soviet pressure, having held Gaza for four months.
3–12 Nov 1956
Khan Yunis and Rafah massacres
1948–1966
Military government over Palestinian citizens of Israel
Eighteen years of permits, curfews, closed areas and administrative detention applied to citizens on the basis of nationality. Abolished in November 1966, five months before the occupation of the West Bank and Gaza began.
25 Jul 1960
Basic Law: Israel Lands bars the sale of state and JNF land
The Basic Law prohibits transferring ownership of land held by the state, the Development Authority or the Jewish National Fund — together about 93 per cent of the territory of Israel. Read with the JNF's own constitution, under which its land is held for the Jewish people, it is the instrument that converted the expropriations of 1948–1953 into a permanent tenure regime.
12 May 1961
Childers publishes 'The Other Exodus' in The Spectator
Erskine Childers examined the BBC and United States monitoring records of every Middle East broadcast of 1948 and reported 'not a single order, or appeal, or suggestion about evacuation from Palestine from any Arab radio station, inside or outside Palestine, in 1948'. He had asked the Israeli Foreign Ministry for the texts in 1958 and was promised them; none was supplied. Rebuttal 20.
28 May 1964
The PLO is founded in Jerusalem
The first Palestinian National Council convened in East Jerusalem, then under Jordanian rule, and adopted the Palestinian National Charter. The organisation was created under Arab League auspices and was taken over by the guerrilla factions after 1967, with Yasser Arafat elected chairman in 1969.
23 Oct 1965
Israel's Supreme Court bars the Socialists' List from the Knesset election
EA 1/65 Yeredor. The Court upheld the exclusion of a list drawn from the banned al-Ard movement, although the Basic Law: The Knesset then contained no provision permitting it, on the reasoning that a purpose denying the state's existence is an unlawful purpose. The first judicial limit on the political rights of Palestinian citizens; Justice Haim Cohn dissented.
1 Dec 1966
Military rule over Palestinian citizens of Israel is abolished
For eighteen years the Palestinians who remained inside the 1949 armistice lines and held Israeli citizenship lived under a military government: permits to travel between towns, curfews, administrative detention and closed areas, administered under the Defence (Emergency) Regulations inherited from the Mandate. It ended eight months before the same regulations were applied to the West Bank and Gaza, where they remain in force.
5–10 Jun 1967
Six-Day War; occupation of WB, Gaza, Golan, Sinai
10–11 Jun 1967
The Moroccan Quarter of Jerusalem is demolished
Within days of the city's capture, 135 houses were bulldozed and about 650 residents expelled to clear the plaza in front of the Western Wall. Two mosques and a zawiya were destroyed. The demolition preceded any decision on the status of the territory and set the pattern for the administrative annexation of East Jerusalem that followed on 28 June.
Jul 1967
Allon Plan sets the template for settlement of the occupied territory
Proposed retaining the Jordan Valley and a chain of settlements; the geography of Area C follows it closely.
27 Aug 1967
Military Order 101 criminalises assembly, procession and publication
The order made a gathering of ten or more persons on a political matter a criminal offence without a permit from the military commander, along with printing, displaying or distributing material of political significance. It remains in force, and is the instrument under which Palestinian protest in the West Bank has been prosecuted for more than fifty years.
1 Sep 1967
The Khartoum summit answers with the three noes
The Arab League summit resolved on no peace with Israel, no recognition and no negotiations, while also resolving to seek withdrawal by political means. The resolution is cited as proof that no partner existed; the same document's commitment to a political rather than military course is usually left out of the citation.
Sep 1967
Theodor Meron advises the Israeli government that civilian settlement violates the Fourth Geneva Convention
The Foreign Ministry's own legal counsel, in a memorandum to the Prime Minister's office: 'civilian settlement in the administered territories contravenes the explicit provisions of the Fourth Geneva Convention.' Settlement proceeded regardless. The strongest single admission in the settlement record.
27 Sep 1967
Kfar Etzion established — the first settlement in the occupied West Bank
Followed by more than 300 settlements and outposts and, by 2026, over 700,000 settlers.
22 Nov 1967
UN Security Council Resolution 242 emphasises the inadmissibility of the acquisition of territory by war
The cornerstone of every subsequent negotiation, and the provision the ICJ relied on in its 2024 advisory opinion.
21 May 1968
Security Council Resolution 252 declares the measures in Jerusalem invalid
The Council held that all legislative and administrative measures taken by Israel to change the status of Jerusalem, including expropriation of land and property, are invalid and cannot change that status, and called on Israel to rescind them. It was the first of a line of resolutions saying the same thing, none of which was complied with.
21 Aug 1969
Arson at Al-Aqsa Mosque; the Organisation of the Islamic Conference is founded in response
The fire, set by an Australian Christian acting alone on a millenarian conviction, destroyed Saladin's 12th-century minbar. UN Security Council Resolution 271 of 15 September 1969 condemned Israel's failure to observe earlier resolutions on Jerusalem; twenty-five states met at Rabat on 25 September and founded the OIC.
Jul–Aug 1971
Sharon's clearance operations in the Gaza refugee camps
Thousands of homes demolished to drive wide roads through the camps for military access; some 16,000 people displaced a second time.
6–25 Oct 1973
October War
Ended the post-1967 assumption that the territorial status quo was costless; it did not alter the status of the occupied territory.
Feb 1974
Gush Emunim is founded to settle the occupied territory
The movement organised the settlement of the West Bank as a religious obligation, beginning with repeated unauthorised attempts at Sebastia near Nablus. Successive governments treated its faits accomplis as facts to be regularised rather than offences to be removed, and its outposts became the template for the process still running.
25–28 Oct 1974
The Rabat summit names the PLO the sole legitimate representative
The Arab League summit recognised the PLO as the sole legitimate representative of the Palestinian people, ending Jordan's claim to negotiate for the West Bank. It is the decision that made the PLO the address for any settlement, and the reason the 1988 Jordanian disengagement followed.
22 Nov 1974
General Assembly Resolution 3236 affirms the right to self-determination and return
The Assembly reaffirmed the inalienable rights of the Palestinian people to self-determination, national independence and sovereignty, and the right of the displaced to return to their homes and property, and granted the PLO observer status. Arafat had addressed the Assembly on 13 November.
10 Nov 1975
General Assembly Resolution 3379 equates Zionism with racism
Adopted by 72 votes to 35 with 32 abstentions, and revoked on 16 December 1991 by Resolution 46/86 as the price of Israel's participation in the Madrid conference. The episode is recorded here because both the adoption and the revocation are regularly cited without the other.
30 Mar 1976
Land Day: six Palestinian citizens of Israel killed protesting land expropriation in the Galilee
The first general strike by Palestinian citizens of Israel, and the event that marked their emergence as a political community within the state.
Sep 1976
The Koenig Memorandum is leaked
Written in April 1976 by Yisrael Koenig, the Interior Ministry's district commissioner for the north, the memorandum proposed measures to dilute the Arab population of the Galilee: expanding Jewish settlement, restricting Arab employment and study, and weakening Rakah. Its publication in Al HaMishmar confirmed that demographic management was departmental policy rather than inference.
May 1977
Likud's founding platform claims sovereignty between the sea and the Jordan
The platform of the party that won the 1977 election stated that "between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty" — rendered in the CIA's archived contemporaneous translation of Ma'ariv as "Israel sovereignty alone will prevail between the sea and the Jordan River". The 1999 platform rejected the establishment of a Palestinian state west of the Jordan. Neither has been rescinded.
1977–1978
Likud takes power; the Drobles Plan sets out settlement to prevent territorial contiguity
The World Zionist Organisation plan stated the aim of settling between and around Palestinian population centres so as to make a contiguous Palestinian territory impossible.
22 Mar 1979
Security Council Resolution 446 calls the settlements a violation of the Fourth Geneva Convention
The Council determined that Israel's settlement policy in the territories occupied since 1967 has no legal validity and constitutes a serious obstruction to peace, and established a commission to examine the situation. The United States abstained; the resolution passed 12–0.
26 Mar 1979
Egypt–Israel peace treaty
Removed the strongest Arab military constraint on Israeli action in the occupied territory; the autonomy talks on the Palestinians led nowhere.
22 Oct 1979
The Elon Moreh judgment bars requisition of private land for settlement
In Dweikat v. Government of Israel (HCJ 390/79) the Supreme Court held that private Palestinian land could not be seized for a settlement founded on ideological rather than military grounds, and ordered Elon Moreh removed. The state's response was to switch to declaring land public — by 1984 more than 800,000 dunams had been declared state land, and settlement continued on it.
30 Jul 1980
Jerusalem Law annexes East Jerusalem
Declared null and void by UN Security Council Resolution 478 in August 1980; the annexation has never been recognised in international law.
20 Aug 1980
Security Council Resolution 478 declares the Jerusalem Law null and void
The Council held the Basic Law a violation of international law, declared it null and void, and called on states with diplomatic missions in Jerusalem to withdraw them. Thirteen states did. The resolution is the standing legal answer to the 2017 American recognition.
1981
Illegal annexation of Golan Heights
8 Nov 1981
Military Order 947 establishes the Civil Administration
The order created a civil administration in the West Bank under the area commander, separating civil functions from the military government in form while leaving them subordinate to it in substance. It is the body that now issues building permits, demolishes houses and administers land in Area C.
17 Dec 1981
Security Council Resolution 497 declares the annexation of the Golan without effect
Adopted unanimously three days after the Golan Heights Law, the resolution held Israel's imposition of its law and administration on the occupied Syrian Golan null and void and without international legal effect, and demanded its rescission. The annexation stands, and was recognised by the United States in March 2019.
6 Jun 1982
Invasion of Lebanon
An estimated 17,000–20,000 killed, the great majority civilians, during the invasion and the siege of Beirut.
16–18 Sep 1982
Sabra and Shatila massacre
16 Dec 1982
The General Assembly calls the Sabra and Shatila massacre an act of genocide
Resolution 37/123 D resolved that the massacre was an act of genocide, by 123 votes to none with 22 abstentions. It is the only occasion on which the Assembly has applied the term to conduct in this conflict, and it did so three months after the killings and while the Kahan Commission was still sitting.
8 Feb 1983
Kahan Commission finds Ariel Sharon personally responsible for Sabra and Shatila
Israel's own commission of inquiry found he bore personal responsibility for ignoring the danger of bloodshed. He resigned as Defence Minister and became Prime Minister eighteen years later.
Feb 1984
The Karp Report is released, two years after it was written
A committee under Deputy Attorney General Yehudit Karp examined seventy cases of settler violence against Palestinians in 1980–1982 and found fifty-three unsolved, with the police failing to investigate and the army and settlers obstructing. The report was submitted in 1982 and withheld; Karp resigned when nothing was acted on, and the findings have been repeated by every subsequent monitor.
4 Aug 1985
The 'iron fist' policy in the occupied territory
Under Defence Minister Yitzhak Rabin: administrative detention restored, deportations resumed on a large scale, and house demolition extended beyond cases of killing or wounding. The UN Special Committee to Investigate Israeli Practices recorded the escalation at the time. Rabin was quoted as saying the purpose was to make life so difficult that the population would leave. The First Intifada began two years later.
9 Dec 1987
First Intifada begins
Dec 1987
Hamas is founded in Gaza
Formed out of the Muslim Brotherhood's Gaza branch in the first days of the intifada. Israel had permitted and in part encouraged the Brotherhood's charitable and religious network through the 1970s and 1980s as a counterweight to the secular nationalism of the PLO — a calculation Israeli officials have since acknowledged in print.
Jan 1988
The 'force, might and beatings' policy in the First Intifada
Defence Minister Yitzhak Rabin's instruction; some 1,000 Palestinians hospitalised with fractures in the first months, documented at the time by Israeli and international medical organisations.
31 Jul 1988
Jordan severs its legal and administrative ties to the West Bank
King Hussein's address dissolved the Jordanian parliament's West Bank seats and ended the administrative link, leaving the PLO as the sole claimant. The Palestinian Declaration of Independence followed in November, and the West Bank's residents were left holding no citizenship at all.
15 Nov 1988
Palestinian Declaration of Independence accepts partition on the 1967 lines
The PLO's acceptance of a state on 22% of historic Palestine. Recognised within a year by more than 80 states.
8 Oct 1990
Al-Aqsa compound killings: 21 Palestinians shot dead
UN Security Council Resolution 672 condemned the killings; Israel refused entry to the Secretary-General's mission.
30 Oct 1991
The Madrid conference opens
The first direct multilateral talks, convened by the United States and the Soviet Union. The PLO was excluded by Israeli veto and the Palestinians attended inside a joint Jordanian delegation. The conference produced no agreement; the channel that produced Oslo was opened secretly alongside it.
17 Dec 1992
415 Palestinians are deported to Marj al-Zuhour
The mass expulsion of alleged Hamas and Islamic Jihad members to a strip of southern Lebanon, where they were refused entry and spent a winter in tents. The Security Council condemned it as a violation of the Fourth Geneva Convention in Resolution 799 the following day and demanded their safe and immediate return; most returned in 1993.
13 Sep 1993
Oslo I: Declaration of Principles signed in Washington
Deferred Jerusalem, refugees, settlements and borders to final-status talks that never concluded. The settler population roughly doubled during the Oslo years.
25 Feb 1994
Cave of the Patriarchs massacre - Goldstein
29 Apr 1994
The Paris Protocol binds the Palestinian economy to Israel's
The economic annex of the Oslo framework created a customs union in which Israel collects Palestinian import duties and VAT and transfers them, sets the tariff schedule, and controls the crossings. The clearance revenues so collected have been withheld repeatedly since, which is why the arrangement is described as a lever rather than a partnership.
4 May 1994
The Gaza–Jericho Agreement begins the transfer of civil powers
Signed in Cairo, it created the Palestinian Authority and moved Israeli forces out of Jericho and most of Gaza, while leaving settlements, borders, airspace and external security with Israel. The interim arrangements it set up for five years are still in force.
28 Sep 1995
Oslo II divides the West Bank into Areas A, B and C
Area C, some 60% of the West Bank and the location of nearly all its land reserves, remained under full Israeli control — an arrangement intended to last five years and still in force after thirty.
4 Nov 1995
Yitzhak Rabin assassinated by Yigal Amir
Killed by an Israeli religious-nationalist opposed to the Oslo Accords, after a campaign of incitement in which Benjamin Netanyahu was a prominent figure.
18 Apr 1996
Qana massacre
17 Jan 1997
Hebron Protocol divides the city into H1 and H2
80% of Hebron transferred to the Palestinian Authority; the 20% containing the Old City, the Ibrahimi Mosque, four settlements and Shuhada Street kept under Israeli control, where some 7,000 Palestinians live alongside 500–800 settlers and about 1,500 soldiers. The Protocol required Shuhada Street to be reopened; it was fenced off entirely in 2002 and Palestinians remain barred from it. Smotrich announced the Protocol's cancellation in June 2026.
23 Oct 1998
Wye River Memorandum
A further redeployment agreement brokered at Wye Plantation; implementation was suspended by Israel two months later. The last of the Oslo-era agreements before the collapse at Camp David.
6 Sep 1999
The Supreme Court holds that the interrogation methods are unlawful
In PCATI v. State of Israel (HCJ 5100/94) the court ruled that shaking, the shabach position, painful squatting and sleep deprivation were not authorised by law, while leaving open a necessity defence for individual interrogators after the fact.
