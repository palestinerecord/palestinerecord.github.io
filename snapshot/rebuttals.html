The 23 arguments that are made in defence of the conduct documented here, each one answered on its own legal terms and with the figures that settle it. The answers are Part XVI of the report, reproduced word for word. Open a claim, read the answer, copy it.

01 “Israel has the right to self-defence after October 7.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The ICJ ruled in 2004 (Wall Advisory Opinion, paragraph 139) that Article 51 of the UN Charter does not apply to attacks emanating from territory under the responding state's effective control as the occupying power. Gaza was under Israeli occupation and siege. Article 51 self-defence is therefore not available.
  • Even where self-defence is available, it is bound by the principles of distinction, proportionality, precaution, and military necessity. Killing more than 20,000 children, destroying all 12 universities, bombing every hospital, and engineering famine is not "self-defence." It is the actus reus of genocide and war crimes.
  • Israel was funding Hamas - its own intelligence service confirmed this contributed to October 7. A state cannot fund an organisation, allow it to grow as a deliberate strategy, then claim "self-defence" when that organisation attacks.
  • Netanyahu was warned, and the money went up anyway. The warnings of a coming attack were specific and they reached him: Sinwar's plan for a "zilzal" ("earthquake") was relayed to the Shin Bet on 15 September 2023, and UAE President Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed telephoned Netanyahu directly roughly ten days before 7 October to warn that Hamas was preparing a "major operation" (Haaretz, 8 September 2026, from the forthcoming book by Shlomi Eldar and Ruti Yuval; Netanyahu's office calls the report an "absolute lie," the UAE has not denied the call, and former PM Naftali Bennett says he "knew for a fact the report is true"). In that same month - September 2023 - Israel asked Qatar to increase the payments to Hamas (Haaretz, January 2026; Ynet; Times of Israel). Netanyahu also did not raise the bin Zayed call at the 1 October 2023 security discussion on Gaza, and both the Shin Bet chief and the IDF Chief of Staff say they were never told of it (§18.7). A state that is warned at head-of-state level, increases the funding, withholds the warning from its own security chiefs, and then invokes self-defence is not describing a surprise attack; it is describing its own policy.
  • The Genocide Convention contains no exception for prior provocation. Two wrongs do not make a genocide lawful.
  • October 7 itself is condemned. Hamas committed war crimes on October 7 - the deliberate killing of civilians and the taking of civilian hostages were unambiguously unlawful. That does not give Israel permission to commit a worse crime.

02 “Hamas uses civilians as human shields. The civilian deaths are Hamas's fault.” Answer

Refutation:

  • Amnesty International investigated extensively and found no evidence that Hamas directs civilians to shield military assets - in 2009, 2014, and 2023–24.
  • The 2009 UN Goldstone Report found no evidence of Hamas ordering or systematically directing civilians to act as shields.
  • Human Rights Watch investigations have not found evidence of a systematic Hamas human-shields strategy.
  • Even if true, international law does not permit indiscriminate response. The presence of a military objective in a civilian area does not lift the duty to take precautions and assess proportionality. Additional Protocol I, Article 51(5)(b) and Article 57 remain binding regardless of the defending force's conduct.
  • Gaza is one of the most densely populated territories on earth (~5,500 people/km²; Jabaliya reaches ~100,000/km²). Any military force operating in Gaza operates in civilian terrain by physical necessity - not because Hamas has positioned civilians there as a strategy.
  • Israel itself systematically uses Palestinian civilians as human shields - documented by Israel's own courts, Israel's own soldiers, and multiple major international investigations. This is not a counter-accusation. It is a documented, judicially condemned, and officially investigated fact:
  • Israel's Supreme Court (HCJ 3799/02, 6 October 2005) unanimously banned the IDF's use of Palestinian civilians as human shields ("neighbor procedure"), establishing that the practice had been occurring at institutional scale since at least 2002. This is Israel's own judicial finding that Israel was systematically using human shields.
  • CNN (24 October 2024): At least 11 IDF squads deployed human shields across five Gaza cities under the "mosquito protocol." Palestinian detainees - including Mohammad Saad, 20 (shot through the back), and Mohammad Shbeir, 17 - were forced to enter booby-trapped buildings before soldiers.
  • Haaretz (August 2024): IDF Chief of Staff Lt. Gen. Herzi Halevi was aware of the practice. An anonymous IDF officer estimated human shields were used "at least six times a day" in Gaza, with "almost every platoon" keeping a Palestinian detainee for this purpose (termed "shawish"). Palestinian detainees were dressed in IDF fatigues (minus shoes) and sent ahead of troops as first-contact targets.
  • AP / Breaking the Silence (May 2025): The Associated Press documented the practice as systemic based on seven Palestinian civilians' accounts and two Israeli soldiers' direct admissions. Breaking the Silence executive director Nadav Faiman: "not isolated accounts... systemic failure and a horrific moral collapse." "Nearly every platoon used a Palestinian to clear locations" - confirmed by a named officer.
  • Haaretz (February 2025): An 80-year-old Gaza civilian was forced to inspect houses for booby traps for eight hours with an IED command-wire tied around his neck. After release, he and his wife were shot dead by a different IDF battalion that had not been informed they were civilians.
  • Jenin jeep incident (June 2024): Mujahed Azmi was strapped to the hood of an IDF military vehicle after being shot during a Jenin raid. UN Special Rapporteur Francesca Albanese: "human shielding in action." US State Department: "absolutely unacceptable." IDF: "violated military protocol"; investigation launched.
  • Children: Two brothers aged 12 and 13 were forced to march in front of tanks in Gaza City (December 2023). The UN Secretary-General's Report on Children and Armed Conflict (June 2025) formally verified 27 Palestinian boys used as human shields by IDF: 5 in the West Bank, 22 in Gaza. Al-Haq et al. documented children as young as nine used as human shields (Urgent Appeal, 27 June 2025).
  • IDF Military Police Criminal Investigation Division opened formal investigations into at least six cases in March 2025 - acknowledging "reasonable suspicion" of systematic criminal conduct. As of May 2026, no charges have been filed.
  • US intelligence (November 2025): Reuters reported US intelligence from 2024 showing Israeli officials discussing soldiers sending Palestinians into booby-trapped tunnels - intercepts shared with the White House.
  • The asymmetry is precise and judicially documented: Hamas has never been found by any independent body to have systematically directed Palestinian civilians to serve as shields. The IDF has been found by its own Supreme Court to have done exactly that. The human shields argument, which is deployed to justify 75,000+ Palestinian deaths, is not only legally irrelevant - it is an accusation that applies more directly to the party making it. See also Section 5.8 and Section 19.5.

11 “Israel issues evacuation warnings; it cannot be accused of deliberately targeting civilians or forcible transfer.” Answer

Refutation:

  • Evacuation orders without a safe destination do not constitute lawful precaution under international law. Additional Protocol I, Article 58 requires parties to "take all feasible precautions" to remove civilians from military objectives. The operative word is "feasible." Ordering 1.9 million people to move to zones that are subsequently bombed does not satisfy this standard.
  • There was nowhere safe to evacuate to. The UN documented repeated Israeli airstrikes on designated "safe zones" - including al-Mawasi, Khan Younis, and Deir al-Balah - after they had been publicly designated safe. The very areas to which civilians were evacuated were subsequently attacked.
  • The UN, Amnesty International, and Human Rights Watch have each found that evacuation orders issued without safety guarantees, return guarantees, or a secure destination constitute forcible transfer under Rome Statute Article 7(1)(d) - a crime against humanity. Evacuation orders are not a legal shield where the evacuation is not genuinely voluntary, reversible, and safe.
  • Israeli officials' own statements establish the intent. Defence Minister Yoav Gallant announced in February 2025 that displaced Palestinians "would not be allowed to return" for at least one year. Prime Minister Netanyahu's May 2025 Knesset statement described the destruction of homes as designed to ensure "Gazans will choose to emigrate outside of the Strip." The evacuation orders are not precaution - they are the instrument of displacement.
  • The ICJ's 24 May 2024 Provisional Measures Order required Israel to immediately halt its military operation in Rafah in part because the evacuation conditions did not meet the legal standard. Israel defied that order and continued the Rafah operation.

12 “Israel lets the aid in. Hamas steals it, and that is why Gazans are hungry.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The United States government's own review found no evidence for the claim. An internal analysis by USAID's humanitarian bureau, completed in late June 2025, examined 156 incidents of theft or loss of United States-funded humanitarian supplies reported by implementing partners between October 2023 and May 2025. It found no reports alleging that Hamas had benefited from any of them, and recorded that under 1 per cent of the aid was affected by loss, theft, diversion, fraud or waste (Reuters, 25 July 2025; corroborated by CNN, NBC News and CBC). The review's own stated limitation is that it could not assess whether recipients had been vetted - so it establishes the absence of evidence of systematic diversion, not proof that no diversion ever occurred.
  • Israeli military officials reached the same conclusion internally. The New York Times reported that the Israeli military had found no proof of systematic theft by Hamas from the United Nations-run aid operation in Gaza - the operation whose replacement the diversion claim was used to justify.
  • The mechanism built on the claim produced mass killing at the distribution points. The Gaza Humanitarian Foundation began distributing on 26 May 2025, replacing the roughly 400 distribution points the UN-coordinated system had operated during the ceasefire with four militarised sites run by armed private United States contractors inside Israeli-controlled perimeters. OHCHR recorded the killing of people trying to collect food near those sites and along convoy routes: 613 by 27 June 2025, 798 by 7 July, 1,373 by 31 July, and more than 2,146 by 4 September 2025. The New Humanitarian put the cumulative toll at at least 2,957 killed and 19,866 wounded by 15 September 2025. More than 165 humanitarian organisations called for the scheme to be shut down; it wound up its operations in November 2025.
  • The obligation does not turn on the recipients' politics. Article 55 of the Fourth Geneva Convention requires the occupying power to ensure the food and medical supplies of the population to the fullest extent of the means available to it; Article 59 requires it to permit relief schemes where the population is inadequately supplied. The ICJ's orders of 26 January and 28 March 2024 required Israel to ensure the unimpeded provision of basic services and humanitarian assistance at scale. Suspicion of diversion is not an exception recognised by any of these instruments.
  • The two questions are separate, and conflating them is the argument's only function. Theft of relief supplies by an armed group is a punishable offence by that group. The deliberate restriction of food to a civilian population by the power that controls every crossing is the conduct for which the ICC issued arrest warrants on 21 November 2024 - starvation of civilians as a method of warfare. A finding on the first would not extinguish the second.

13 “There is no famine in Gaza. The IPC is repeating Hamas figures.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The IPC classification is a technical determination against fixed thresholds, not a casualty count supplied by a party. Famine (IPC Phase 5) is classified only where at least 20 per cent of households face an extreme lack of food, at least 30 per cent of children are acutely malnourished, and at least two deaths per 10,000 people per day are occurring from starvation or from the interaction of malnutrition and disease. The inputs are household surveys, anthropometric screening of children and mortality data, assessed by an independent Famine Review Committee.
  • The determination was made and has not been displaced. On 22 August 2025 the IPC confirmed famine in Gaza Governorate as of 15 August 2025 - the first famine classification ever made in the Middle East and the first outside Africa. It recorded over 500,000 people in catastrophic conditions (Phase 5), 1.07 million (54 per cent of the population) in emergency conditions (Phase 4) and 396,000 in crisis (Phase 3), and projected the famine to extend to Deir al-Balah and Khan Younis by the end of September 2025. Northern Gaza could not be classified at all, for lack of access. The WHO confirmed the finding the same day. The IPC projected 132,000 children acutely malnourished in the period to June 2026.
  • The rebuttal offered no data. Israel's Ministry of Foreign Affairs responded that "there is no famine in Gaza" and that the report was "based on Hamas lies laundered through organisations with vested interests"; COGAT's head, Major General Ghassan Alian, called the sources "partial and unreliable sources, many of them affiliated with Hamas". Neither statement produced an alternative survey, an alternative mortality series, or access for independent assessment to the northern governorates the IPC had been unable to classify. A denial that also withholds the means of verification is not a rebuttal of the finding; it is a second fact about the party denying it.
  • The response of the other states on the Security Council. Fourteen of the fifteen members issued a joint statement on 27 August 2025 on the famine determination. The United Nations humanitarian chief, Tom Fletcher, described the evidence as "irrefutable testimony", adding: "It is a famine, the Gaza famine."
  • Famine is not a natural disaster in an occupied territory whose crossings are controlled by the occupying power. Where the population's access to food is regulated by a single authority, a famine classification is evidence going to the conduct of that authority, which is why starvation as a method of warfare is the charge on the ICC warrants of 21 November 2024 and why the ICJ's provisional measures concerned aid access specifically.

15 “'From the river to the sea' is a call for the destruction of Israel, and therefore incitement to genocide.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The formulation's best-documented governmental use is Israeli. The founding platform of Likud in 1977 stated that "between the Sea and the Jordan there will only be Israeli sovereignty" - a rendering preserved in the CIA's archived translation of Ma'ariv, May 1977, as "Israel sovereignty alone will prevail between the sea and the Jordan River". Likud's 1999 platform rejected the establishment of a Palestinian state west of the Jordan. Neither has been rescinded.
  • It remains the stated position of the serving prime minister. At a press conference on 18 January 2024, Benjamin Netanyahu said he "will not compromise on full Israeli security control over all the territory west of the Jordan River", repeating a formulation he had used since 2017, and set it out in his "day after" plan of February 2024.
  • The consequences run one way. The phrase in the mouth of a legislator or a student has produced a congressional censure and university suspensions of student organisations, including Jewish Voice for Peace and Students for Justice in Palestine at Columbia. The same territorial claim in a governing party's platform, and in the prime minister's own words, has produced no consequence at all. A phrase whose punishment depends on who says it is not being treated as incitement; it is being treated as a permit.
  • Incitement to genocide is a defined offence, and the bodies applying that definition did not find it in a chant. Article III(c) of the Genocide Convention requires a direct and public call to commit the acts enumerated in Article II, made with genocidal intent and assessed in its full context - the standard applied by the ICTR in Prosecutor v. Akayesu (1998) and Nahimana et al. (2003). Applying it to the record of this war, the UN Commission of Inquiry found direct and public incitement to genocide in the statements of President Herzog, Prime Minister Netanyahu and former Defence Minister Gallant - officials with command of the means. It made no such finding about a slogan, and the slogan is capable of describing a single democratic state between the river and the sea, which is what many of those using it say they mean.

16 “Israel is being singled out. Why is there no comparable campaign over Syria, Yemen, Sudan or China?” Answer

Refutation:

  • At the level of enforcement the record is the opposite of singling out. Since October 2023 the United States has vetoed seven Security Council drafts concerning this conflict - 18 October 2023 (Brazil's draft on humanitarian pauses), 8 December 2023 (the UAE draft after the Secretary-General invoked Article 99), 20 February 2024 (Algeria's draft), 18 April 2024 (Palestinian UN membership), 20 November 2024, 4 June 2025 and 18 September 2025 (the elected members' ceasefire drafts, each carried by 14 votes to 1). No other situation on the Council's agenda has been shielded on this scale in this period.
  • The findings were made by the bodies with jurisdiction, applying the instruments they apply everywhere else. The ICJ's Advisory Opinion of 19 July 2024 held Israel's continued presence in the occupied territory unlawful, required it to be brought to an end as rapidly as possible, and held that third states must not render aid or assistance in maintaining it. That is an application of the ordinary law of occupation and self-determination, not a special rule. The same is true of the ICC warrants, which issue on the Rome Statute's elements, and of the Genocide Convention findings.
  • This record applies the same standard to the other party. Part XVIII and §18.10 set out Hamas's war crimes on 7 October 2023 - the killing of civilians, hostage-taking and sexual violence - under the identical instruments, and record its killings and torture of Palestinians. A standard that is applied to both parties in the same document is not a standard applied selectively.
  • The duty to prevent genocide is not comparative. Under Bosnia and Herzegovina v. Serbia and Montenegro (2007) the obligation arises for a state party the instant it learns of a serious risk, and is breached where the state "manifestly failed to take all measures to prevent genocide which were within its power". Nothing in Article I makes the obligation conditional on equivalent action elsewhere. Whataboutery is not a defence known to the Convention; it is an account of why the obligation was not discharged.
  • The complaint is usually made by the parties with the greatest capacity to act. The states most often raising selectivity are the principal suppliers of the weapons and the diplomatic cover. Selectivity in enforcement is a real defect of the international system, and it is an argument for enforcing more, not for enforcing nothing here.

17 “Arab citizens of Israel vote, sit in the Knesset and serve on the Supreme Court, so the apartheid charge collapses.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The finding concerns the whole territory under a single authority, most of whose Palestinian residents cannot vote for it. Roughly three million Palestinians in the West Bank live under Israeli military law, tried in military courts, unable to vote for the government that controls their movement, land, water and residency; a further two million in Gaza have lived under closure since 2007. The crime of apartheid is defined by the structure of domination over the territory as a whole, not by the franchise available to a minority inside the Green Line.
  • Even inside that franchise, the constitutional text is explicit. Basic Law: Israel - the Nation State of the Jewish People (19 July 2018) provides that the right to exercise national self-determination in the state "is unique to the Jewish people", declares Jewish settlement "a national value" that the state will act to encourage and promote, and removed Arabic's status as an official language. A constitutional provision that assigns self-determination to one group by name is the institutional form the ICSPCA and Rome Statute Article 7(2)(h) describe.
  • The Supreme Court on which the objection relies upheld it. On 8 July 2021 an extended bench of eleven justices rejected fifteen petitions against the Basic Law by ten to one, President Esther Hayut holding that it did not negate the state's democratic character. The sole dissent came from Justice George Karra, the court's only Arab justice. Formal representation did not produce a different constitutional outcome; it produced a single dissenting vote.
  • The ordinary legislation runs the same way. Adalah's Discriminatory Laws Database records more than 65 laws that discriminate directly or indirectly against Palestinian citizens of Israel and Palestinians in the occupied territory. The Citizenship and Entry into Israel Law (Temporary Order, 2003), which bars family unification where the spouse is a Palestinian from the West Bank or Gaza, was renewed annually for 22 years - a "temporary" measure applied to one group for a generation.
  • The legal test does not ask whether every right is denied. Article II of the 1973 Convention and Article 7(2)(h) of the Rome Statute define apartheid as an institutionalised regime of systematic oppression and domination by one racial group over another, committed with the intention of maintaining it. The presence of voters, legislators or a judge from the dominated group is consistent with that definition, as it was in the jurisdiction the Convention was written for. The question is who holds the power to determine the status of the other group, and whether that determination is structural. On the record set out in Part IX, it is.

18 “There is no genocide. Israel is fighting Hamas, and there is no intent to destroy the Palestinians as such.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The legal test is an inference from the record, not a confession. In Bosnia and Herzegovina v. Serbia and Montenegro (26 February 2007) the ICJ held that genocidal intent may be established from a pattern of conduct where genocide is "the only inference that could reasonably be drawn from the acts in question" (paragraph 373). No order has ever had to be produced for a genocide to be found. In this case the statements exist as well: the UN Independent International Commission of Inquiry found that President Isaac Herzog, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu and former Defence Minister Yoav Gallant committed direct and public incitement to genocide, a separate offence under Article III(c) of the Convention, and Appendix C of this report reproduces the statements verbatim.
  • "In whole or in part" is the text of the instrument. Article II requires intent to destroy a national, ethnical, racial or religious group "in whole or in part". The ICTY Appeals Chamber in Prosecutor v. Krstić (19 April 2004) confirmed that the targeted destruction of a substantial part of a group within a limited geographical area satisfies the definition - at Srebrenica, some 8,000 men and boys over several days. The observation that Palestinians continue to exist in Ramallah, Amman or Chicago is therefore not an answer to the charge; it is a misstatement of what the charge is.
  • The determination has been made by the bodies that make it. The UN Commission of Inquiry on the OPT found genocide on 16 September 2025 (chair Navi Pillay) and again on 23 June 2026 (chair Srinivasan Muralidhar, on the deliberate targeting of children); the UN Special Committee to Investigate Israeli Practices in November 2024; Amnesty International in December 2024; the International Association of Genocide Scholars by an 86 per cent vote on 31 August 2025; the Lemkin Institute for Genocide Prevention and Genocide Watch, whose July 2026 report places Gaza at Stages 8, 9 and 10 simultaneously. The ICJ found on 26 January 2024 that it was plausible that the conduct falls within the Convention and ordered binding provisional measures; the ICC issued arrest warrants on 21 November 2024 for starvation of civilians as a method of warfare.
  • The Israeli and Jewish sources close off the deflection. The finding is not an external one. The Israeli organisations B'Tselem ("Our Genocide", July 2025) and Physicians for Human Rights Israel (July 2025) reached it independently; the Israeli Holocaust historians Raz Segal and Omer Bartov and the Hebrew University historian Amos Goldberg voted for it; and a former Prime Minister of Israel, Ehud Olmert, wrote in Haaretz on 18 June 2026 that what is being carried out is a state-funded campaign of ethnic cleansing and crimes against humanity. An argument that only hostile outsiders use the word cannot survive its own source list.
  • The conduct stands on the record whatever it is called. A total blockade of food, water, fuel, medicine and commercial goods from 2 March 2025, the first famine ever classified in the Middle East (IPC, 22 August 2025), the destruction of every university and the great majority of the housing stock, and the figures this record carries and updates daily, are documented facts before any label is applied to them. The label determines which court has jurisdiction. It does not determine what happened.
  • The duty to prevent does not wait for the judgment. Under Bosnia (2007) the obligation arises "at the instant that the State learns of, or should normally have learned of, the existence of a serious risk that genocide will be committed", and is breached where a state "manifestly failed to take all measures to prevent genocide which were within its power". The ICJ's own order of 26 January 2024 is that trigger. A state that waits for the merits judgment - not expected before 2028 - has not been cautious; it has inverted a convention whose entire purpose is prevention into a licence to watch.

19 “Hamas broke the ceasefire.” Answer

Refutation:

  • In the ceasefire of January 2025 the sequence is documented, and it does not run that way. The agreement was sealed on 15 January 2025 and entered into force on 19 January. Phase one ran 42 days, to 1 March 2025; its exchanges of hostages and prisoners were carried out in eight rounds and completed. The negotiations on phase two, which the agreement required to open on the sixteenth day, were never opened by Israel. On 2 March 2025 Israel halted the entry of all goods into Gaza, beginning the total blockade. On 18 March 2025, without warning and while phase one's terms were still being observed, it resumed bombing, killing more than 400 people in a single night - the deadliest day since October 2023.
  • The same pattern is on the record from the previous war. The Egyptian-brokered tahdiya of June 2008 held until the Israeli raid of 4 November 2008, which killed six Hamas members; the rocket fire resumed after it, and Operation Cast Lead began on 27 December 2008. A ceasefire ended by the party that then names the other as the breaker is not an isolated event in this record.
  • The current ceasefire has been broken continuously, and in one direction. The agreement was reached on 8 October 2025 and took effect on 10 October 2025; the Security Council endorsed the framework in Resolution 2803 of 17 November 2025. Since it took effect Israel has killed 1,344 Palestinians and injured 4,464 in Gaza (Gaza Ministry of Health and OCHA, to 10 September 2026). Phase two remains unopened, deadlocked on disarmament and on withdrawal from the territory still held behind the so-called yellow line.
  • Reciprocity is not a defence in the law of armed conflict. Common Article 1 of the Geneva Conventions binds each party "in all circumstances", and Additional Protocol I, Article 51(6) prohibits attacks on the civilian population by way of reprisals. There is no rule under which one party's violation licenses the other's. A ceasefire breach is a fact about a ceasefire; it is not a defence to starvation, to indiscriminate attack, or to genocide.
  • This record states Hamas's breaches too. Part XVIII sets out the war crimes of 7 October 2023 under the identical instruments, including the taking of civilian hostages, which is a grave breach and is itself a continuing violation for as long as hostages are held. The point is not that one party is faultless. It is that "they broke the ceasefire" is being offered as an answer to a charge sheet that does not depend on who broke it.

20 “The Arabs rejected partition in 1947, and the refugees left in 1948 because Arab leaders told them to.” Answer

Refutation:

  • What was rejected should be stated before the rejection is judged. UN General Assembly Resolution 181(II) of 29 November 1947 allotted about 55 to 56 per cent of Mandate Palestine to a Jewish state, to a population that was about one third of the total and held registered title to roughly 7 per cent of the land. The proposed Jewish state would itself have contained an Arab population nearly equal to its Jewish one. A General Assembly resolution is a recommendation, not a binding instrument; declining a recommendation is not an offence, and no rule of law converts it into a forfeiture of the right of self-determination.
  • The expulsions began before any Arab state entered the war. Plan Dalet was issued on 10 March 1948 and provided in terms for "destruction of villages (setting fire to, blowing up, and planting mines in the debris)" and for expulsion where resistance was met. Deir Yassin was attacked on 9 April 1948 by the Irgun and Lehi, with over a hundred villagers killed. Haifa's Palestinian population was driven out on 21-23 April, Jaffa's by 13 May. The regular armies of the Arab states crossed on 15 May 1948. Roughly 300,000 Palestinians had already been displaced by that date.
  • The claim about radio orders was tested against the broadcast archive, and it failed. The Irish journalist Erskine Childers, writing in The Spectator on 12 May 1961 ("The Other Exodus"), examined the BBC and United States monitoring records of every Middle East broadcast of 1948 and reported that there was "not a single order, or appeal, or suggestion about evacuation from Palestine from any Arab radio station, inside or outside Palestine, in 1948", and that the monitored records contained repeated Arab appeals to civilians to stay where they were. He had asked the Israeli Foreign Ministry in 1958 for the texts, the dates or the station names behind the claim, and was promised them; none was ever supplied. Walid Khalidi reached the same conclusion in 1959. The Israeli historian Benny Morris, working from the Israeli military archives (The Birth of the Palestinian Refugee Problem Revisited, 2004), found no blanket evacuation order and documented expulsion orders, massacres and psychological warfare as causes of flight.
  • The outcome was made permanent by legislation, which is the part the story is told to obscure. About 750,000 Palestinians were displaced, more than 500 villages were depopulated and destroyed, and by 1949 the new state held 78 per cent of Mandate Palestine. The Absentees' Property Law (1950) then vested the property of anyone absent on the relevant dates in a Custodian, including that of "present absentees" who had never left the country's new borders. UN General Assembly Resolution 194(III) of 11 December 1948, paragraph 11, resolved that refugees wishing to return should be permitted to do so at the earliest practicable date; it has been reaffirmed annually since and never implemented.
  • Even if the claim were true, it would establish nothing. Flight does not extinguish title, and no rule of international law makes the right of return conditional on the reason for departure. Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention prohibits forcible transfer and requires the return of transferred persons as soon as hostilities in the area cease. A property right that could be defeated by proving that its owner ran from a battle would not be a property right.

21 “They were offered a state at Camp David and turned it down. They never miss an opportunity to miss an opportunity.” Answer

Refutation:

  • Israel's own foreign minister at Camp David rejected this account. Shlomo Ben-Ami, who led the Israeli delegation, told Democracy Now! on 14 February 2006: "If I were a Palestinian I would have rejected Camp David, as well." He has written that Camp David offered no sovereign, contiguous state. Ben-Ami is not a partisan of the Palestinian case and says so in the same exchange - he holds Yasser Arafat responsible for the later response to the Clinton Parameters, and adds that "Taba is the problem" - which is why the concession matters: the specific claim about Camp David is denied by the Israeli minister who led the delegation.
  • The talks did not end at Camp David, and what ended them was an Israeli election. The Taba negotiations of January 2001 came closest, and the EU special representative Miguel Moratinos's agreed non-paper records substantially narrowed gaps on territory, refugees and Jerusalem. They were broken off by Prime Minister Ehud Barak for the election campaign, and Ariel Sharon was elected on 6 February 2001 on a platform of not resuming them. The negotiations were not refused; they were discontinued by the party that had convened them.
  • The 2008 offer was withdrawn by the man who made it, and could not be examined. On 16 September 2008 Ehud Olmert proposed that Israel retain 6.3 per cent of the West Bank against a 5.8 per cent land swap, with a link to Gaza and the Old City placed under an international regime. He showed Mahmoud Abbas the map but would not let him take a copy away; an aide reproduced it from memory on a napkin. Olmert had announced in July 2008, under criminal investigation, that he would step down, and submitted his resignation on 21 September 2008 - five days after the meeting - continuing only as caretaker until the next government took office. An offer that cannot be studied, and that its author has no remaining authority to deliver, is not an offer that can be accepted or refused.
  • What did not pause for any of the negotiations was the taking of the thing being negotiated. The settler population of the West Bank excluding East Jerusalem rose from about 110,000 in 1993 to nearly 200,000 by 2000, and stands today at over half a million across the occupied territory. A negotiation in which one party continues to acquire the subject matter is not an offer; it is a timetable.
  • The current government has removed the premise. Netanyahu stated on 18 January 2024 that he would not compromise on "full Israeli security control over all the territory west of the Jordan River", and on 18 July 2024 the Knesset adopted a resolution by 68 votes to 9 rejecting the establishment of a Palestinian state in any circumstances, expressly including as part of a negotiated settlement. The resolution is not binding, which is the point: it is the legislature stating its position where nothing turned on it. The claim that the offer remains open and is being refused is contradicted by the legislature of the state said to be making it.
  • Self-determination is not conditional on accepting an offer. The ICJ's Advisory Opinion of 19 July 2024 held the continued Israeli presence in the occupied territory unlawful and required it to be brought to an end as rapidly as possible, without making that obligation contingent on a negotiated outcome. Occupation is not a lawful default that ends only when the occupied population signs.

22 “Boycotting Israel is antisemitic.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The call is a demand for compliance with three legal findings, and it says so. It was issued on 9 July 2005 - the first anniversary of the ICJ's Wall Advisory Opinion - by more than 170 Palestinian civil society organisations, and it asks for three things: an end to the occupation of the land seized in 1967 and the dismantling of the Wall (the ICJ's own holding of 9 July 2004), full equality for Palestinian citizens of Israel, and respect for the right of return under UN General Assembly Resolution 194. Each is a claim in law about a state's conduct. None of them is a claim about Jews.
  • The European Court of Human Rights has decided the point. In Baldassi and Others v. France (applications nos. 15271/16 and six others, Fifth Section, 11 June 2020) the Court held unanimously that the criminal conviction of eleven activists for calling in supermarkets for a boycott of Israeli goods violated Article 10 of the European Convention, and awarded each applicant €7,380. It held that a call to boycott is a form of expression on a matter of public interest and that convicting for it required reasons that were relevant and sufficient, which France had not given. The Court expressly distinguished Willem v. France (2009), where the applicant was a mayor announcing a boycott in the exercise of public authority - the distinction being the use of state power, not the opinion expressed.
  • The equivalent domestic authority runs the same way. In NAACP v. Claiborne Hardware Co., 458 U.S. 886 (1982), the United States Supreme Court held that a politically motivated boycott is protected speech under the First Amendment; several federal courts have since enjoined state anti-boycott certification requirements on that basis.
  • Israel's own legislation treats it as political, not racial. The Law for Prevention of Damage to the State of Israel through Boycott passed the Knesset on 11 July 2011 by 47 votes to 38, creating civil liability for calling for a boycott of the state or of the settlements. In Avneri v. Knesset (HCJ 5239/11, 15 April 2015) an expanded bench struck down only section 2(c), which had allowed damages without proof of harm, and upheld the rest, including the application of the law to boycotts of the settlements alone. A state that legislates civil remedies against a political campaign is answering a political campaign. It is not answering a hate crime, for which it already has criminal law.
  • The tactic's own history refutes the charge, and so does the list of those using it. The identical instrument was directed at apartheid South Africa with the support of the General Assembly and is now universally treated as legitimate. Among those endorsing the Palestinian call today are Jewish Voice for Peace, the largest Jewish-American anti-Zionist organisation, and Israeli academics and citizens. A definition of antisemitism that classifies a Jewish organisation's boycott of a state as hatred of Jews has stopped describing the prejudice and started describing the policy. See Part XIV and §21.4 on the same substitution.

23 “Jews are indigenous to the land, so Zionism cannot be settler-colonialism.” Answer

Refutation:

  • The two propositions are not alternatives, and the criteria being invoked do not say what they are said to say. The working definition used in the UN system is José Martínez Cobo's (Study of the Problem of Discrimination against Indigenous Populations, 1986): historical continuity with pre-invasion societies, non-dominance in the present, and self-identification. It describes a group's present relationship to a dominant power, not a hereditary title to territory. Palestinians include the descendants of the land's continuously resident population - among them Jews, Christians and Muslims who never left. Ancestry does not settle the question, because the question the framework asks is who is dominated now.
  • The movement described its own project in the vocabulary now said not to apply to it. Theodor Herzl wrote in Der Judenstaat (1896) that the Jewish state would "form a portion of a rampart of Europe against Asia, an outpost of civilization as opposed to barbarism"; he approached Cecil Rhodes in 1902 for endorsement and the Colonial Office in 1903 over Uganda. The institutions were named accordingly: the Jewish Colonization Association (1891), the Palestine Jewish Colonization Association, and the Zionist Organisation's own colonisation departments.
  • The clearest statement of the settler-colonial analysis is a Zionist one. Ze'ev Jabotinsky, "The Iron Wall", published on 4 November 1923: "Zionist colonisation must either stop, or else proceed regardless of the native population. Which means that it can proceed and develop only under the protection of a power that is independent of the native population - behind an iron wall, which the native population cannot breach." He added that "every native population in the world resists colonists as long as it has the slightest hope of being able to rid itself of the danger of foreign settlement". Rashid Khalidi's The Hundred Years' War on Palestine (2020) traces the programme from that text forward.
  • The founding instrument was a European promise about a territory whose inhabitants were not consulted. The Balfour Declaration of 2 November 1917 was issued by a power that did not yet hold the territory, on behalf of a population then under a tenth of it. Balfour's own memorandum of 11 August 1919 put the position without ambiguity: "in Palestine we do not propose even to go through the form of consulting the wishes of the present inhabitants of the country ... Zionism, be it right or wrong, good or bad, is rooted in age-long traditions, in present needs, in future hopes, of far profounder import than the desires and prejudices of the 700,000 Arabs who now inhabit that ancient land."
  • However the historical question is answered, the legal one does not turn on it. No rule of international law converts ancestral connection into title, and none makes it a defence to the rules that actually apply: the prohibition on the acquisition of territory by force (UN Charter Article 2(4); Security Council Resolutions 242 and 2334), the prohibition on an occupying power transferring its own civilian population into occupied territory (Article 49(6), Fourth Geneva Convention), and the right of self-determination the ICJ applied on 19 July 2024. If antiquity of claim licensed the displacement of present inhabitants, the rule would be universal, and no state now existing would be secure in its borders. It is not a rule, and it has never been one.
