Since the ceasefire

What stopped and what did not

A ceasefire announced on 29 September 2025 entered into force on 10 October 2025. It ended the phase of the war that was killing tens of thousands a year. It did not end the killing, and it did not end the siege: on 6 June 2026 every crossing was closed again. In the 348 days since, the Ministry of Health has recorded 1,344 Palestinians killed under a ceasefire — and its register of the identified dead has risen by 6,711, which is 9.1 per cent of the whole toll of the war, as bodies are recovered and identifications completed. The register is growing at 19.3 a day against 91.3 a day before the ceasefire; the killings the Ministry counts in the period work out at about 3.9 a day. On either measure the killing slowed sharply. On neither did it stop.

The register and the count of killings since the ceasefire are two different figures. The register moves when an identification is completed, so it carries bodies recovered from the rubble months after the strike that killed them, and it rises faster than the number of people killed in the period. The chart plots the register; the figures above are the Ministry's and UNICEF's counts of what was done after 10 October 2025.

Israel expanded its territorial control inside Gaza from the 53 per cent envisaged under the agreement to about 70 per cent of the Strip by July 2026, by moving the Yellow Line westward. OCHA records about 200 Palestinians killed near those shifting lines. Sections 6.7 to 6.10 of the report set out the conduct of this period in full: the strikes that continued, the closure of every crossing on 6 June 2026, and the talks that ran alongside both.

Who the dead are

Age and sex of the identified dead

Not a model and not an estimate: the Gaza Ministry of Health names list, 72,835 individual identification records, each with a name, an identity number, a date of birth and a sex. Of those, 21,637 were under eighteen and 0 carry no usable age. This is the dataset that makes the claim of an overwhelmingly combatant death toll checkable, and it does not survive the check.

The true toll

Every recorded figure is a floor, not a ceiling

The Gaza Ministry of Health count is a register of identified bodies, not an estimate of deaths. Three methodologically independent studies — statistical list-overlap modelling, household survey, and demographic reconstruction — converge on the same conclusion: it undercounts by roughly a third to two-fifths, and it excludes the dead still under the rubble.

The Gaza Mortality Survey estimated a further 16,300 non-violent excess deaths over the same period — deaths from the collapse of the health system, water and food supply rather than from weapons. Total estimated mortality on that basis is approximately 83,650. The widely circulated figure of 186,000 comes from a 2024 correspondence letter in The Lancet applying an indirect-to-direct multiplier of 4:1 drawn from other conflicts; it is a projection of eventual total war-attributable mortality, not a body count, and is presented here as such. The Costs of War Project at Brown University has published in the same terms.

Under the Genocide Convention the legal question is the conduct and the intent, not the arithmetic. But the arithmetic matters to the public record: every figure quoted in this dashboard from the Ministry of Health is, on the evidence of three independent studies, a floor and not a ceiling.
