Children

The children’s record, 1948 to the present

Across every period this record can count from a single source that counted both sides on one methodology, 22,237 Palestinian children and 185 Israeli children were killed. That is 120.2 to 1.

The argument

Why does this matter?

Because a child is not a combatant, cannot be one, and is the one category of person about whom the two sides of this argument agree. Every other figure in this record can be met with a claim about who was fighting whom. This one cannot.

And because the ratio settles the question the rhetoric is built to avoid. When 99.2 per cent of the children killed are killed by one party, the question of who is defending whom has an answer, and it is not the answer that party gives. You cannot claim to be defending a child while killing 120 others.

That formulation is not this record’s. It is the argument countingthekids.org has made since 2012, from its own count of the years 2000 to 2014, where the ratio ran at about eleven to one. This record does not reproduce their figure; it computes its own, from its own sourced series, and the second-Intifada window below — 11.8 to 1 — is where that earlier claim can be checked.

How this page counts

Three windows are counted in full. Each is taken from a single source that counted both sides on one methodology, so the ratio inside a window is a comparison the source itself supports. Periods no source counted are published as gaps. Figures that cover only one side, or only one territory, are marked partial and no total depends on them.

Definition. A child is a person under 18, except in the B’Tselem window, where the published table is cut at under 17 and the Palestinian figure is therefore a floor.
