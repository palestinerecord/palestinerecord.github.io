Since 1948

Seventy-eight years of the death toll, and the holes in it

Everything else on this dashboard runs on a daily or monthly series that begins in October 2023. This block carries the periods before it. No continuous Palestinian death register has ever existed, so the record is assembled from separate bodies of work — a contemporaneous Palestinian enumeration of 1948, B'Tselem's case-by-case database from 1987, OCHA's monitoring from 2005, the Gaza Ministry of Health register from 2023 — each with its own definitions, geography and start date.

Every figure below carries the organisation that produced it and the method it used. Where no organisation published a figure, the row carries a constructed estimate instead: it is labelled as an estimate, shown with its low and high bounds, set out with the components it was built from, and excluded from the documented running total. The blocks are not additive with one another unless marked as counted, because several of the series overlap in time and none of them count the same population.

Treat every number here as a floor. Undercounting is structural: bodies never recovered, deaths in detention never recorded, indirect deaths from siege and the destruction of the health system, and twenty years of occupation for which no organisation was systematically counting at all.

The occupation before the First Intifada
The longest hole in the record, and not an accident of archiving: it covers the two decades in which the settlement enterprise was built. It also contains events Israel's own institutions have adjudicated — the Kahan Commission found Defence Minister Ariel Sharon personally responsible for Sabra and Shatila, and he became Prime Minister eighteen years later. The First Intifada
1,087 killed by the Israeli army, 107 killed by Israeli civilians in the occupied territories (94 in the West Bank, 13 in Gaza), and 28 killed inside Israel. 240 were minors. In the first thirteen months alone, 332 Palestinians were killed against 12 Israelis. The report records the same period as over 1,000 killed including 237 children, and Defence Minister Yitzhak Rabin's explicit order to break the limbs of stone-throwing children; Rabin received the Nobel Peace Prize in 1994. The Second Intifada and the decade after it
6,371 Palestinians killed by Israeli security forces. 1,317 were minors. At least 2,996 were not participating in hostilities when they were killed, against 2,193 who were, with 694 unclassified — so of the cases where status could be established, the majority were not fighting. 248 were Gaza police killed in the opening minutes of Cast Lead and 240 were targets of assassination. Three of the nine periods have no published figure at all, and they span 1967–1987 and 1993–2000 — twenty-seven years. The absence is itself a finding: the record is thinnest exactly where there was no occupying-power obligation to publish, no Palestinian state to count, and no international monitoring mission in place. Rather than leave the three rows empty, each carries a constructed estimate with its arithmetic shown in full, marked as an estimate wherever it appears and never added to the documented running total.

136,000 — Palestinians killed since 1948

Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (Dr Ola Awad), World Refugee Day report, June 2024

The only cumulative figure published by the Palestinian statistical authority. It was issued in June 2024, when the Gaza toll stood near 37,000, so it implies roughly 99,000 deaths across 1948–2023 — far more than the individually documented periods below sum to. PCBS has not published the period-by-period basis for it, so it cannot be reconciled line by line with the blocks in this table. It is shown as an anchor, not as a total.

  • The funnel is not a backlog. Most complaints are closed without a criminal investigation ever being opened, so the case never enters the system that could convict anyone.
  • This matters legally, not just morally. Complementarity under the Rome Statute means the International Criminal Court may act only where the state is unwilling or unable genuinely to investigate. A 0.9% indictment rate over nine years is the evidentiary basis for that finding, produced by an Israeli organisation from Israeli military records.
  • Yesh Din's figures cover soldiers only. Israeli civilians who attack Palestinians in the West Bank are handled by the Israel Police, whose own closure rates over the same period are of the same order.
Displacement and demolition

The continuous mechanism

Killing is the most visible part of the record and the smallest part of it by number. Dispossession is the continuous mechanism, and it runs through every period above without a break.

  • ICAHD's figure for the occupied territory is published in three versions — 26,000, 48,038 and 55,000 — which differ by date and by what counts as a structure, not by disagreement about the events. The highest is its February 2019 compilation and is the one shown here.
  • ICAHD's own methodological warning applies to every figure in this block: neither Israel nor OCHA keeps a count of home demolitions separately from the demolition of structures, so barns, animal pens, cisterns and fencing are inside the same totals. ICAHD also states that it is still investigating demolitions carried out between 1967 and 1982, before the Civil Administration began keeping records.
  • Demolition is administrative, not military. The stated ground in the large majority of cases is building without an Israeli permit, in a permit system that almost never issues one to a Palestinian while approving thousands of settlement housing units over the same period.
Mass detention

Since 1967, without charge

The occupation's other continuous instrument. Israel has held Palestinians under military order in the occupied territory since 1967, and administrative detention — indefinite imprisonment on secret evidence, without charge or trial — has been available to it throughout.
