The Hannibal Directive

What Israeli fire did on 7 October

The Hannibal Directive authorises lethal force to stop Israelis being taken captive, including force against the captives. It was formulated in 1986, rescinded in 2016, and reactivated on the morning of 7 October 2023.

What Israeli fire did on 7 October

Every bar is a documented count from the Israeli record — Haaretz, Channel 12, the IDF’s own statement. The grey bar at nought is the number of autopsies establishing which weapon killed each victim.

Before the attack

The policy of keeping Hamas funded

Successive Israeli governments treated a strong Hamas in Gaza as the guarantee against a Palestinian state. The record is Israeli: the Wall Street Journal, Haaretz, the Shin Bet's own investigation, and Netanyahu in his own words.

The policy of keeping Hamas funded

The bars are the documented monthly rate of $15 million annualised, not a published total; they are drawn dashed for that reason, and the 2023 bar is a part year. A pin marks a year in which something is on the record beyond the money.

The record, in order

  • 1982 — Israel's military governor funds the network
    Brigadier General Yitzhak Segev, military governor of Gaza in the early 1980s, said he had personally funded the Islamist welfare network that became Hamas, as a counterweight to the secular PLO (Wall Street Journal, 24 January 2009).
  • 2012 — Netanyahu to Dan Margalit
    It was important to keep Hamas strong as a counterweight to the Palestinian Authority (Israel Hayom, December 2012).
  • 2018 — The suitcases begin
    Netanyahu's government approves monthly Qatari cash payments of $15 million and more, hand-delivered through Rafah with explicit Israeli approval.
  • 2019 — Netanyahu states the strategy
    To his Likud Knesset members, March 2019: 'Anyone who wants to thwart the establishment of a Palestinian state has to support bolstering Hamas and transferring money to Hamas.' Ehud Barak confirmed the strategy was to keep Hamas 'alive and kicking'.
  • 2023 — Israel asks Qatar to increase the funding
    September 2023, one month before the 7 October attack, following Hamas threats of escalation (Haaretz, January 2026; corroborated by Ynet and the Times of Israel).
  • 2023 — The warnings reach Netanyahu, and the policy does not change
    Sinwar's plan for a 'zilzal' ('earthquake') was relayed to the Shin Bet on 15 September 2023, and UAE President Sheikh Mohammed bin Zayed telephoned Netanyahu directly roughly ten days before 7 October to warn that Hamas was preparing a 'major operation' (Haaretz, 8 September 2026, from the book by Shlomi Eldar and Ruti Yuval). Netanyahu's office calls the report an 'absolute lie'; the UAE has not denied the call; former Prime Minister Naftali Bennett says he knew for a fact the report is true. Netanyahu did not raise the call at the 1 October 2023 security discussion, and the Shin Bet chief and the IDF Chief of Staff both say they were never told of it. The funding request and the warnings fall in the same month; the published record does not fix their order, and the policy was not reversed after the warnings arrived.
  • 2025 — The Shin Bet's own finding
    4 March 2025: the Shin Bet's internal investigation found Qatari funds reaching Hamas's military wing, through a pipeline that circumvented Israeli civilian-aid oversight, were among the factors that enabled preparation for 7 October. The probe flagged Netanyahu's facilitation of the funding and his opposition to operations against senior Hamas leaders. Qatar disputes the characterisation, stating no funds reached Hamas's political or military wing.

The record states monthly Qatari cash payments of $15 million and more, hand-delivered through the Rafah crossing with explicit Israeli government approval, continuing for years from 2018. The yearly bars are that monthly rate annualised — the rate is documented, the totals are this dashboard's arithmetic, and the true figure is higher because the record says '$15 million+'.

Qatar's position is recorded alongside the Shin Bet's: it states that no funds reached Hamas's political or military wing.

How the targets were chosen

Three systems, disclosed by the officers who operated them

Three systems, disclosed by the Israeli intelligence officers who operated them: one that lists people, one that lists buildings, and one that waits until the person is inside his own house.

The AI targeting systems

Log scale. The four rows share one unit — human beings — which is why they can sit on one axis. The dashed bar is the error rate applied to the list size: both figures are the investigation’s, the multiplication is this dashboard’s.

§6.4

Interactive chart — open it in the live record.

The three systems

  • Lavender — Lists people
    Officers were given sweeping authorisation to adopt the system's kill lists with no requirement to verify its reasoning.
  • Habsora ("The Gospel") — Lists buildings
    Identifies structures alleged to serve a military function and generates high-volume bombing target lists.
  • Where's Daddy? — Waits for the house
    Tracks a targeted individual to his family home and enables the strike when he is inside it, which guarantees the deaths of the spouse, the children and the neighbours.

Officers spent as little as twenty seconds on a target before authorising a bombing, primarily to confirm the target was male.

  • The systems were documented by Israeli intelligence officers speaking to +972 Magazine, not by the targets or their families.
  • The Lieber Institute at West Point and multiple international law scholars have raised whether AI targeting used in this manner inherently violates the principles of distinction and proportionality.

Lebanon

Forty-four years of the same doctrine

The Dahiya Doctrine is not an inference drawn by critics. It was set out by Major General Gadi Eisenkot, later IDF Chief of Staff, after the 2006 war: "What happened in the Dahiya quarter of Beirut in 2006 will happen in every village from which Israel is fired on… We will apply disproportionate force on it… From our standpoint, these are not civilian villages, they are military bases." What follows is the record of its application.

Beyond Gaza

Operations on other states' territory

In three calendar years, Israel conducted offensive military operations on the sovereign territory of at least four states: Lebanon, Syria, Yemen and Iran. Under Article 2(4) of the UN Charter, members shall refrain from the use of force against the territorial integrity of any state; under Article 51 the only exception is self-defence against an armed attack. Almost none of what follows was authorised by the Security Council.
